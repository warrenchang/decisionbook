Appendix A — Portable Tools

Use these tools as printable work surfaces, not as another sequence to memorize. They are grouped by the decision task. Each tool points back to a mechanism in the main text and produces an inspectable output.

Individual judgment and decision process

NoteOne-Page Decision Journal

Decision and deadline; alternatives considered; best feasible alternative forgone; predictions with probabilities; values and trade-offs; assumptions; emotional and social context; review date. After the outcome: compare forecast with result, separate process from luck, and state what should and should not change.

NoteDecision Audit Studio

Part 1 — capture the story. In five sentences, state the decision, choice, why it seemed right at the time, what happened, and how good the decision now seems on a 1–7 scale.

Part 2 — expose the process. Reconstruct alternatives, predictions, valuation, opportunity cost, context or state, and intended feedback. Identify the weakest link. Rate process quality and outcome quality separately. Finish: “The weakest link was ___ because . Process quality: /7. Outcome quality: /7. Next time I will improve it by .”

The audit cannot prove that the chosen road was best or recover the counterfactual road not taken. It can make missing options, weak evidence, hidden values, social influence, and unresolved uncertainty visible.

NoteAttention Audit

What captured attention? What was missing? Which question, role, metric, order, device, or time pressure made that pattern likely? What design would make the neglected variable visible?

NotePrediction–Valuation Split

Write the disputed claim twice. Under Prediction, state what each person believes will happen, with probability or range and the evidence that could change it. Under Valuation, state what matters, to whom, the consequence if the prediction is wrong, and the trade-off each person is willing to accept. Finish with one sentence: “We disagree mainly about , so the next useful step is .”

NoteExpectation Loop Map

Choose a belief about yourself, another person, a group, a market, or a negotiation. Map: belief → attention → interpretation → emotion or physiology → action → response from others or environment → feedback. Mark the strongest confirmation point and one intervention point. Ask whether the final evidence was independent or partly produced by the original expectation.

NoteBias Redesign Studio

Choose one process—hiring, investing, exam preparation, project planning, purchasing, or negotiation. Name a likely trigger such as an early anchor, vivid anecdote, identity threat, global impression, or time pressure. Redesign the process before the trigger appears: specify who does what, when, using which independent evidence. Prefer a changed environment to a promise of willpower.

NoteHeuristic Detector
  1. What target question should be answered?
  2. What easier question did the mind appear to answer?
  3. Which cue made that substitution tempting?
  4. When would that cue be ecologically useful?
  5. What denominator, comparison, or disconfirming evidence is needed here?

Finish with a redesign that changes the judgment process, not merely a warning to “be less biased.”

Probability, risk, time, and well-being

NoteProbability Judgment Audit

Define the target event, reference class, time horizon, and conditioning direction. Write the complement. For AND questions, check whether a detailed conjunction has been made more likely than one of its parts; for OR questions, check overlap before adding. Translate percentages into natural frequencies with a common denominator. Separate correlation from a causal claim. Record the forecast, probability, resolution rule, and later Brier score.

NoteRisky-Choice Audit

List outcomes, probabilities or ambiguity, expected value, utility-relevant consequences, and the maximum affordable loss. Elicit the certainty equivalent before naming a risk attitude. State the reference point and whether the same prospect was described as a gain or a loss. Ask whether insurance, diversification, or an information purchase changes the distribution. Finish by naming what expected utility explains, what prospect theory describes, and what neither model identifies from one choice.

NoteExperience-Sample Audit

What outcomes were encountered, how many times, and through which sampling policy? Which rare outcomes could easily be absent? What caused sampling to stop? Are recent, vivid, or similar cases overrepresented? Did earlier choices remove opportunities for corrective learning? Add the best available description—base rates, denominators, time horizon, and uncertainty—then explain why description and experience agree or conflict.

NoteIntertemporal MOST Audit

Map the complete outcome stream for every option. Check Magnitude, Opportunity cost and hidden zeros, Sign, and Time frame. Then test liquidity, future-payment reliability, inflation, transaction costs, subjective time, future-self distance, and anticipation. Compare within-option integration with across-attribute comparison. Use commitment only when it protects a goal the chooser endorses and preserves a proportionate recovery path.

NoteMental-Accounting Audit

Name the label, reference point, budget boundary, opening and closing date, and payment–consumption link. Separate acquisition utility from transaction utility and sunk costs from future consequences. Reconstruct the choice once inside the current account and once across the full balance sheet or portfolio. Preserve a boundary when it protects a valued goal; relax it when the label, rather than future value, drives the decision.

NoteMarket Claim Audit

State the information set and expected-return model behind an efficiency claim. For an anomaly, ask about risk, mispricing, data mining, transaction costs, financing, short-sale constraints, and post-publication decay. For a bubble claim, separate uncertain fundamental value from evidence of feedback, resale motives, leverage, liquidity, and arbitrage limits. Historical patterns are evidence to diagnose, not trading instructions.

NoteStrategic Interdependence Map

Identify players, actions, payoffs, information, timing, repetition, and enforcement. Mark dominant actions, best responses, Nash equilibria, focal points, and mixed motives. Predict what each player believes the other will do—and what each thinks the other believes. Keep one-shot reasoning, repeated-game incentives, social preferences, norms, and evolutionary selection as distinct candidate mechanisms.

NoteWell-Being Measurement Audit

Specify whether the claim concerns experienced affect, remembered experience, life evaluation, meaning, or behavior. State the question wording, time window, scale, sample, comparison, and uncertainty. Separate income level from changes and adaptation; correlation from intervention; average effects from distribution and subgroup differences. Use forecasts from comparable lives or episodes before trusting a focusing intuition about what will make life go well.

Behavior and self-regulation

NoteBehavior Redesign Canvas

Target behavior; cue ecology; urge or local problem; current response; immediate reward or relief; smallest replacement; friction change; prompt; immediate reinforcement; measurement; recovery plan; ethical check.

NoteHabit Autopsy

Choose a mild, private repeated behavior. Map contextual cue → response tendency → action → immediate outcome or relief → learning update. Craving may occur, but do not assume it is required. Ask what local problem the behavior solves and what alternative could solve that problem with lower long-term cost.

NoteOne-Minute Urge Observation

Use a mild impulse, not a clinical crisis. Recognize the cue; breathe; allow the sensation to be present; investigate its location, image, emotion, and promise; name it as an experience rather than an identity; choose the next wise action. After sixty seconds, record what changed and what did not. This is practice in observing an urge, not a substitute for professional support where medical or addiction risk is present.

Persuasion, communication, and connection

NotePersuasion Canvas

Audience current model; protected identity; barrier to change; meaningful mismatch; new causal model; credible evidence; appropriate emotion; source credibility; agency-preserving action; uncertainty and counterargument.

NoteModel-Updating Message

Build the audience model before the message: who decides; desired response; current model; main barrier; evidence the audience would accept; and costs, risks, status concerns, or practical constraints. Then draft: current model → meaningful mismatch → candidate model → evidence → feasible action.

For a short form, use AND–BUT–THEREFORE: shared reality, a consequential contradiction, and the necessary next model or action. For a fuller pitch, use STORY: Shared reality; Trouble; Opportunity; Reasons to believe; Yes to a specific decision. A strong Yes names who does what, by when, with what commitment, while preserving meaningful choice.

NotePersuasion Video Studio

Create a 3–5 minute message for a real audience and submit a design note of no more than 200 words: audience’s current model; meaningful BUT; proposed update; strongest evidence; protected value or identity; specific action; uncertainty; and ethical safeguard.

Peer feedback diagnoses the mechanism, not the presenter: Where did shared reality become clear? Which contradiction created a reason to update? What evidence carried the model change? What might trigger resistance? Is the requested action feasible and freely rejectable?

NoteLayered Listening

WHAT happened? HOW did it feel or unfold? WHY does it matter? WHO is implicated in identity or relationship? WHEN is the timing or history relevant? ACTION: what does the person want now - understanding, advice, decision, repair, or commitment?

NoteVerified Understanding Loop

Ask; listen; reflect the meaning in your own words; invite correction; repeat until the speaker can say, “Yes, that is it.” Agreement is optional. Accuracy, dignity, and the speaker’s authority to correct your model are not.

NoteThree Conversation Labs

Connection prediction. Before a four-minute paired conversation, privately predict enjoyment, awkwardness, and connection. Compare forecast with experience. Participation and depth must be voluntary.

Depth and responsiveness. Begin naturally, then ask permission to follow one answer a level deeper—from fact to experience, meaning, emotion, or value. Debrief the difference between curiosity and intrusion.

Warm honesty. In triads, practice care + observable behavior + owned impact + invitation to the other person’s view + one testable request. The observer checks whether the speaker stayed on their side of the net. Unsafe or high-power conflicts may require documentation, representation, or formal support rather than a classroom script.

Negotiation

NoteNegotiation Preparation

BATNA and how to improve it; reservation value; target; opening offer; objective standards; issues and priorities; estimate of their interests and alternatives; questions; packages; concession plan; ethical boundaries; implementation and review.

NoteNegotiation Clip or Case Debrief

Before viewing or reading, predict the central difficulty. Then record: tactic or move; counterpart’s reaction; information learned or lost; economic, relational, or reputational cost; and one better alternative. A clip illustrates behavior; it does not establish effectiveness outside its context.

NoteTactic Diagnostic

Treat a tactic as a possible signal, not proof of the counterpart’s constraint. Diagnose it before reacting.

A diagnostic response to common bargaining tactics
Move What it may be trying to signal or produce Disciplined response
Flinch or visible shock Your proposal is unreasonable Return to standards and ask for the basis of the reaction; emotion is not evidence.
Nibble A late, small request will be conceded because both sides want closure Reopen the package and link any addition to a reciprocal change.
Limited authority An absent approver prevents movement Verify the mandate and make any proposal conditional on approval of the whole package.
Good cop / bad cop Pressure followed by relief should accelerate concession Treat the roles as one process; slow down and ask for constraints and proposals in writing.
Exploding offer or deadline Delay will destroy the opportunity Verify what changes at the deadline, compare the BATNA, and request enough time for informed review.
Take it or leave it The stated term is final Test credibility without bargaining against yourself; compare the complete offer with the BATNA.
Bogey—falsely presenting a low-priority issue as crucial—false scarcity, or a fake bottom line A priority or constraint is more important or fixed than it is Ask for evidence, use conditional packages, and verify claims where possible. Deliberate deception remains unethical even when tactically common.
NoteMediation and Arbitration Are Different Choices

A mediator helps parties communicate, clarify interests, and design a voluntary agreement but ordinarily does not impose the outcome. An arbitrator hears the dispute and issues a decision that may be binding or nonbinding under the agreed procedure. In final-offer arbitration, the arbitrator selects one complete submitted proposal rather than constructing a compromise. Arbitration therefore transfers more outcome control to a third party. Before accepting either process, verify the neutral’s authority, the decision rule, confidentiality, costs, enforceability, and any right of review or appeal.

NotePackage and Contingency Worksheet

List all negotiable issues. For each side, estimate priority, threshold, and the evidence that would update the estimate. Build three multiple equivalent simultaneous offers that are approximately equal in value to you but trade differently across issues. If disagreement concerns the future, ask whether a contingent term can be made measurable, enforceable, resistant to sabotage, and compatible with both parties’ risk capacity. After a signed deal, keep it as the fallback while searching for a voluntary post-settlement improvement.

Organizational choice and decision systems

NoteChoice-Architecture Audit

Trace one real path: objective → menu → understanding → action → feedback. Mark the options, order, categories, default, mapping, friction, timing, social information, and exit. Write the mechanism as a testable sentence: “Changing ___ should affect ___ because ___.” Compare a nudge with a boost, incentive, mandate, or structural change. Check purpose, welfare, evidence, transparency, exit, and distribution of burden.

NoteStructured Judgment Pipeline

Define the decision and target; decompose criteria; use a reference class; collect independent estimates before discussion or AI output; compare and aggregate; run a premortem or strongest-objection test; decide and record assumptions, authority, and exceptions; schedule review. Use more structure when the decision is consequential, repeated, uncertain, difficult to reverse, contested, or partly automated.

NoteVideo Viewing Protocol

Linked videos remain with their rights holders. Use them as cases rather than evidence by themselves. Before viewing, state a prediction. During viewing, record only observable behavior. Afterward, identify the proposed mechanism, a plausible alternative explanation, the hidden cost, one boundary condition, and one action you would test in a real setting.

Part VII Review

These ten questions connect ideas from Chapters 39–42. Use the assumptions given in each question and choose the one best answer. Work through the complete set before checking the explanations.

Practice questions

Choose one answer for each of the 10 questions. Complete the set, then select Check answers to see your score and explanations. Each question is worth one point.

1. A service team is choosing between adding reminders and simplifying its application form. Which finding most strongly favors simplifying the form first?

2. Two people accept the same rain forecast but choose different policies about carrying an umbrella. Which additional information is most relevant before judging either policy mistaken?

3. One intervention sends a reminder at the intended action time. Another is a voluntarily chosen restriction preventing access to a competing activity. Which distinction is correct?

4. A panel's recorded estimates disagree with an AI forecast. Which evidence would most strongly justify assigning the AI greater weight for this type of decision?

5. A redesigned application page increases submissions but also increases processing delays and ineligible applications. Which outcome is most useful for deciding whether to retain it?

6. A team has resources to pilot a new decision aid while continuing the current workflow. Which design best isolates the aid's effect on the same predefined performance measure?

7. A reader wants to make routine exercise more enjoyable at the time it occurs. Which arrangement uses temptation bundling?

8. A model recommends denying a request, but its training labels reflect past administrative decisions that may contain errors. What is the most important governance step?

9. A protective action costs 3 and prevents an event loss of 20 completely. There are no other effects. Group A has event probability 0.1 and group B 0.3. Under expected-loss minimization, which policy fits?

10. A program has one available place and the same cost for either recipient. Reliable causal estimates say it reduces A's event risk from 40% to 35% and B's from 25% to 10%. If the sole objective is to maximize expected events prevented, who should receive the place?

0 of 10 answered

Answers and explanations

  1. Question 1: C

    A. Users complete the form easily when a reminder arrives before the deadline. (Incorrect)
    This suggests that a prompt can help initiate an otherwise feasible action.
    B. Users report that they planned to apply but forgot the closing date. (Incorrect)
    Forgetting the deadline makes reminders a more direct candidate.
    C. Users repeatedly return but stop at a field they cannot interpret. (Correct)
    Repeated attempts combined with a specific comprehension barrier point to task difficulty rather than a missing prompt.
    D. Users complete the form quickly after a colleague mentions the service. (Incorrect)
    Successful completion after a prompt provides little evidence of a form-comprehension barrier.
    E. Users rarely open the existing reminder because it arrives during the night. (Incorrect)
    This points toward the timing of the prompt rather than the form's difficulty.

    Review: Chapter 39 · Chapter 40. Back to question 1

  2. Question 2: C

    A. Whether the two people discussed the forecast before making their choices (Incorrect)
    Discussion history does not establish the relevant costs and benefits of either action.
    B. How many other people chose to carry an umbrella that day (Incorrect)
    The majority's policy does not establish what is best for either person's circumstances.
    C. How each person values carrying the umbrella and getting wet (Correct)
    Different consequences can justify different action thresholds under the same probability forecast.
    D. Whether the forecast is displayed as a percentage or as odds (Incorrect)
    Equivalent displays do not determine which consequences each person should prefer.
    E. Which forecasting service has the best reputation among their friends (Incorrect)
    The stem already gives a shared forecast; source reputation does not settle differences in the value of acting.

    Review: Chapter 41 · Chapter 42. Back to question 2

  3. Question 3: E

    A. Both are informational defaults. (Incorrect)
    Neither necessarily preselects a menu option.
    B. Both establish that a habit has already formed. (Incorrect)
    An intervention is not evidence of learned automaticity.
    C. The first removes choice; the second only changes wording. (Incorrect)
    This reverses their practical effects.
    D. The first is a mandate; the second is merely a prediction. (Incorrect)
    The reminder does not mandate, and the restriction changes choices.
    E. The first prompts action; the second is a commitment device. (Correct)
    A reminder supplies a cue, while a restriction changes later feasible behavior.

    Review: Chapter 39 · Chapter 40. Back to question 3

  4. Question 4: E

    A. Closer agreement with panel estimates after members review the AI's explanation (Incorrect)
    Agreement after exposure can reflect anchoring and does not validate either forecast independently.
    B. More detailed explanations of the variables contributing to the AI forecast (Incorrect)
    Detailed explanations may help inspection without establishing predictive performance.
    C. Higher confidence scores accompanying the AI's most recent predictions (Incorrect)
    Self-reported confidence requires calibration against outcomes before it can justify greater weight.
    D. Better fit to the historical cases used to develop the forecasting system (Incorrect)
    Fit on development data can overstate predictive performance on new decisions.
    E. Lower error than the panel’s independent forecasts on comparable held-out cases (Correct)
    A fair comparison uses information available at the time of each forecast. Relevant out-of-sample performance then provides a basis for weighting the forecasts without relying on confidence or presentation.

    Review: Chapter 41 · Chapter 42. Back to question 4

  5. Question 5: C

    A. Satisfaction ratings collected immediately after a form is submitted (Incorrect)
    Immediate experience omits later delays, eligibility outcomes, and service delivery.
    B. The proportion of applicants who return to check their submission status (Incorrect)
    Return visits can reflect uncertainty or delays rather than improved welfare.
    C. Net benefit from services delivered after counting processing and applicant costs (Correct)
    The decision needs a downstream benefit measure that accounts for burdens as well as the rise in activity.
    D. The number of submitted applications per visitor to the redesigned page (Incorrect)
    Conversion measures the proximal action but omits the downstream consequences.
    E. The time users spend locating and pressing the submission button (Incorrect)
    Faster interaction does not establish that services reach eligible applicants efficiently.

    Review: Chapter 39 · Chapter 40 · Chapter 42. Back to question 5

  6. Question 6: E

    A. Compare the aided team's performance with a historical industry benchmark. (Incorrect)
    Differences in case mix and conditions can explain a gap from the benchmark.
    B. Compare volunteers who adopt the aid with colleagues who prefer the current workflow. (Incorrect)
    Adoption choices can be related to ability, motivation, or the cases staff handle.
    C. Ask experienced managers to assess how plausible the aid's recommendations appear. (Incorrect)
    Expert plausibility ratings do not isolate its effect on the predefined performance measure.
    D. Compare performance before and after the whole team switches to the new aid. (Incorrect)
    Other changes over time can contribute to the observed difference.
    E. Randomly assign comparable cases to the two workflows during the same period. (Correct)
    Random allocation and contemporaneous comparison help separate the aid's effect from case selection and changing conditions.

    Review: Chapter 39 · Chapter 41 · Chapter 42. Back to question 6

  7. Question 7: E

    A. Set aside money that will be forfeited if the weekly target is missed. (Incorrect)
    This is a commitment penalty rather than pairing exercise with a tempting activity.
    B. Display a reminder beside the door before the usual exercise time. (Incorrect)
    This supplies a prompt rather than an enjoyable experience during the task.
    C. Record each completed session on a chart shared with a friend. (Incorrect)
    Monitoring and social accountability differ from bundling the activity with an immediate temptation.
    D. Watch a favorite film after completing the week's full exercise target. (Incorrect)
    This is a later contingent reward, rather than enjoyment bundled with performing the activity.
    E. Reserve a favorite entertainment podcast for listening during exercise. (Correct)
    The enjoyable activity is paired with the beneficial task, supplying an immediate reason to begin and continue it.

    Review: Chapter 39 · Chapter 40. Back to question 7

  8. Question 8: B

    A. Treat past labels as correct because they are numerous. (Incorrect)
    Sample size does not establish label validity.
    B. Audit labels and criteria; assign an accountable owner and review route. (Correct)
    Predictions can reproduce historical judgments; authorization and correction remain human responsibilities.
    C. Use a more fluent explanation instead of checking the label process. (Incorrect)
    Presentation cannot repair a flawed target.
    D. Infer that model confidence proves the decision is fair. (Incorrect)
    Confidence does not validate the target or fairness standard.
    E. Remove the appeal route to prevent disagreement with the model. (Incorrect)
    This blocks correction of consequential mistakes.

    Review: Chapter 41 · Chapter 42. Back to question 8

  9. Question 9: D

    A. Randomize equally because neither probability exceeds 0.5. (Incorrect)
    The threshold is 3/20 = 0.15, not one half.
    B. Act for neither group. (Incorrect)
    For B the expected benefit exceeds the cost.
    C. Act for A only. (Incorrect)
    This reverses the comparison of avoided loss with cost.
    D. Act for B only. (Correct)
    Expected avoided losses are 2 for A and 6 for B; only B exceeds the cost 3.
    E. Act for both groups. (Incorrect)
    For A the certain cost exceeds the expected avoided loss.

    Review: Chapter 40 · Chapter 42. Back to question 9

  10. Question 10: B

    A. Neither, because risk is not reduced to zero. (Incorrect)
    Partial risk reduction still serves the stated objective.
    B. B, because the expected reduction is 15 percentage points. (Correct)
    B's estimated benefit exceeds A's five-point reduction.
    C. A, because treated risk remains 35%. (Incorrect)
    Remaining risk does not measure the benefit of treatment.
    D. A, because baseline risk is 40%. (Incorrect)
    Highest baseline risk is not the same as greatest preventable risk.
    E. Either, because the program costs the same. (Incorrect)
    Equal cost does not erase the unequal benefits.

    Review: Chapter 40 · Chapter 42. Back to question 10

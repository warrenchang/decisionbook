Part VII Review

These ten questions connect ideas from Chapters 39–42. Use the assumptions given in each question and choose the one best answer. Work through the complete set before checking the explanations.

Practice questions

Choose one answer for each of the 10 questions. Complete the set, then select Check answers to see your score and explanations. Each question is worth one point.

1. People remember to apply for a service and want it, but cannot complete a required identity check on their phones. Which change best matches the observed barrier?

2. Two teams share the same rain forecast but choose different umbrella policies because carrying an umbrella has different costs for them. What does this illustrate?

3. One intervention sends a reminder at the intended action time. Another is a voluntarily chosen restriction preventing access to a competing activity. Which distinction is correct?

4. A panel records human estimates before seeing an AI forecast. They disagree strongly. Which next step best uses this disagreement?

5. A redesigned application page increases submissions but also increases ineligible applications and processing delays. Which evaluation best assesses the full decision path?

6. A team wants to test a new decision aid. Which plan best supports learning whether it improves the current process?

7. A reader can exercise safely and remembers the plan, but finds sessions dull. She wants to keep flexible timing and avoid financial penalties. Which design best matches these preferences?

8. A model recommends denying a request, but its training labels reflect past administrative decisions that may contain errors. What is the most important governance step?

9. A protective action costs 3 and prevents an event loss of 20 completely. There are no other effects. Group A has event probability 0.1 and group B 0.3. Under expected-loss minimization, which policy fits?

10. A program has one available place and the same cost for either recipient. Reliable causal estimates say it reduces A's event risk from 40% to 35% and B's from 25% to 10%. If the sole objective is to maximize expected events prevented, who should receive the place?

0 of 10 answered

Answers and explanations

  1. Question 1: C

    A. Send more reminders about why the service matters. (Incorrect)
    Memory and motivation are already present.
    B. Assume abandonment reveals lack of interest. (Incorrect)
    The stem states interest and identifies another cause.
    C. Provide a workable identity-check route on the devices people use. (Correct)
    The bottleneck is practical ability at a specific step.
    D. Hide the identity requirement until the final step. (Incorrect)
    Concealment does not make the requirement feasible.
    E. Increase the number of motivational slogans. (Incorrect)
    Slogans do not fix the technical barrier.

    Review: Chapter 39 · Chapter 40. Back to question 1

  2. Question 2: C

    A. Decision costs should be removed from the analysis. (Incorrect)
    Those costs are essential to the choice.
    B. Forecast accuracy determines a unique policy for every person. (Incorrect)
    Different consequences can justify different actions.
    C. A shared prediction can support different decisions when consequences differ. (Correct)
    Action thresholds depend on costs and benefits as well as probabilities.
    D. One forecast must be numerically incorrect. (Incorrect)
    The forecast is shared; valuations differ.
    E. The teams must disagree about the definition of rain. (Incorrect)
    No such disagreement is specified.

    Review: Chapter 41 · Chapter 42. Back to question 2

  3. Question 3: E

    A. Both are informational defaults. (Incorrect)
    Neither necessarily preselects a menu option.
    B. Both establish that a habit has already formed. (Incorrect)
    An intervention is not evidence of learned automaticity.
    C. The first removes choice; the second only changes wording. (Incorrect)
    This reverses their practical effects.
    D. The first is a mandate; the second is merely a prediction. (Incorrect)
    The reminder does not mandate, and the restriction changes choices.
    E. The first prompts action; the second is a commitment device. (Correct)
    A reminder supplies a cue, while a restriction changes later feasible behavior.

    Review: Chapter 39 · Chapter 40. Back to question 3

  4. Question 4: E

    A. Accept whichever estimate is stated with more confidence. (Incorrect)
    Confidence can be miscalibrated.
    B. Average the estimates automatically without inspecting shared errors. (Incorrect)
    Averaging need not resolve a systematic or input error.
    C. Choose the AI because computational complexity establishes validity. (Incorrect)
    Complexity is not a performance test.
    D. Replace the recorded human estimates with their post-AI revisions. (Incorrect)
    This erases the independent starting point needed for the audit.
    E. Check inputs and assumptions, then compare both methods on relevant cases. (Correct)
    Independence makes the disagreement informative; comparative evidence can guide resolution.

    Review: Chapter 41 · Chapter 42. Back to question 4

  5. Question 5: C

    A. Evaluate only whether the button was clicked faster. (Incorrect)
    Click speed is still further from the intended benefit.
    B. Assume higher activity guarantees higher welfare. (Incorrect)
    Activity and welfare are distinct targets.
    C. Track submissions, eligibility, actual service delivery, delays, and user costs. (Correct)
    A proximal action metric can improve while downstream outcomes deteriorate.
    D. Use submission counts as the sole success measure. (Incorrect)
    This ignores the newly identified consequences.
    E. Drop ineligible applications from every report without recording them. (Incorrect)
    Their processing burden remains relevant.

    Review: Chapter 39 · Chapter 40 · Chapter 42. Back to question 5

  6. Question 6: E

    A. Keep only examples where the aid and manager agreed. (Incorrect)
    Agreement-based selection excludes important failures.
    B. Compare easy cases using the aid with hard cases under the old process. (Incorrect)
    Case difficulty is confounded with the aid.
    C. Replace the original records with the aid's rewritten summaries. (Incorrect)
    That obscures the evidence needed to reconstruct performance.
    D. Choose the success metric after seeing which result looks best. (Incorrect)
    Post hoc selection can exaggerate improvement.
    E. Predefine outcomes and randomly assign comparable cases to the two workflows. (Correct)
    Predefined criteria and randomized allocation support a causal comparison of the workflows.

    Review: Chapter 39 · Chapter 41 · Chapter 42. Back to question 6

  7. Question 7: E

    A. Require a forfeitable deposit for missing a scheduled session. (Incorrect)
    This conflicts with the preference against financial penalties and fixed timing.
    B. Repeat instruction on an exercise she already performs correctly. (Incorrect)
    Additional skill training does not target the specified boredom.
    C. Publish a league table of other people's attendance. (Incorrect)
    Social comparison does not directly supply the desired immediate enjoyment.
    D. Add an extra reminder at a fixed time every day. (Incorrect)
    The stated barrier is enjoyment, not forgetting.
    E. Pair exercise with a favorite audiobook reserved for those sessions. (Correct)
    Temptation bundling adds immediate enjoyment without fixed timing or a financial penalty.

    Review: Chapter 39 · Chapter 40. Back to question 7

  8. Question 8: B

    A. Treat past labels as correct because they are numerous. (Incorrect)
    Sample size does not establish label validity.
    B. Audit labels and criteria; assign an accountable owner and review route. (Correct)
    Predictions can reproduce historical judgments; authorization and correction remain human responsibilities.
    C. Use a more fluent explanation instead of checking the label process. (Incorrect)
    Presentation cannot repair a flawed target.
    D. Infer that model confidence proves the decision is fair. (Incorrect)
    Confidence does not validate the target or fairness standard.
    E. Remove the appeal route to prevent disagreement with the model. (Incorrect)
    This blocks correction of consequential mistakes.

    Review: Chapter 41 · Chapter 42. Back to question 8

  9. Question 9: D

    A. Randomize equally because neither probability exceeds 0.5. (Incorrect)
    The threshold is 3/20 = 0.15, not one half.
    B. Act for neither group. (Incorrect)
    For B the expected benefit exceeds the cost.
    C. Act for A only. (Incorrect)
    This reverses the comparison of avoided loss with cost.
    D. Act for B only. (Correct)
    Expected avoided losses are 2 for A and 6 for B; only B exceeds the cost 3.
    E. Act for both groups. (Incorrect)
    For A the certain cost exceeds the expected avoided loss.

    Review: Chapter 40 · Chapter 42. Back to question 9

  10. Question 10: B

    A. Neither, because risk is not reduced to zero. (Incorrect)
    Partial risk reduction still serves the stated objective.
    B. B, because the expected reduction is 15 percentage points. (Correct)
    B's estimated benefit exceeds A's five-point reduction.
    C. A, because treated risk remains 35%. (Incorrect)
    Remaining risk does not measure the benefit of treatment.
    D. A, because baseline risk is 40%. (Incorrect)
    Highest baseline risk is not the same as greatest preventable risk.
    E. Either, because the program costs the same. (Incorrect)
    Equal cost does not erase the unequal benefits.

    Review: Chapter 40 · Chapter 42. Back to question 10

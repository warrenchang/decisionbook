Part VI Review

These ten questions connect ideas from Chapters 35–38. Use the assumptions given in each question and choose the one best answer. Work through the complete set before checking the explanations.

Practice questions

Choose one answer for each of the 10 questions. Complete the set, then select Check answers to see your score and explanations. Each question is worth one point.

1. A job seeker wants more time with family. Her best available option if negotiations fail is keeping her current job. Which pair identifies these two facts?

2. A supplier claims spare capacity but says its full customer list is confidential. Which approach best checks capacity while protecting unrelated sensitive information?

3. On existing terms a seller needs at least €110 and a buyer will pay at most €100. A changed delivery method saves the seller €20 without costing the buyer anything. What is the new price ZOPA if the seller passes the saving into its minimum?

4. A seller makes progressively smaller price concessions on an unchanged package. Which use of this information is most defensible for the buyer?

5. Two parties disagree about a future sales forecast but agree on how sales can be verified. Which possibility should they examine?

6. A negotiator offers several packages she values equally, with different combinations of price, timing, and support. What can the other party's preference reveal?

7. A sales bonus rewards orders entered before month-end, even if customers later cancel them. Which contract revision most directly aligns the bonus with sales retained after cancellations?

8. After signing an acceptable agreement, two parties want to explore whether they have missed opportunities for mutual gain. Which rule best protects both parties during that search?

9. A buyer's alternative costs €500 all-inclusive. Under the proposed deal the buyer pays price p plus €30 transport. The seller incurs €350 in costs only if this deal proceeds; its best alternative provides €90 net. No other differences matter. What price range makes both at least as well off as their alternatives?

10. A buyer values an asset at €150 if demand is high and €70 if low. It believes high demand has probability 0.5; the seller believes 0.8 and needs expected revenue of at least €100. Both are risk neutral. A proposed payment is €130 if high and €50 if low; the buyer's outside payoff is zero. Is this acceptable to both on these criteria?

0 of 10 answered

Answers and explanations

  1. Question 1: E

    A. An opening offer and a target (Incorrect)
    Neither fact specifies a proposed negotiated term.
    B. A ZOPA and a contingent payment (Incorrect)
    Neither an overlap interval nor an outcome-dependent payment is described.
    C. A reservation value and a concession (Incorrect)
    No acceptance limit or change in an offer is supplied.
    D. A BATNA and an interest (Incorrect)
    This reverses the roles.
    E. An interest and a BATNA (Correct)
    Family time is an underlying concern; staying is an outside course of action.

    Review: Chapter 35 · Chapter 36. Back to question 1

  2. Question 2: C

    A. Require the complete customer database before discussing relevance. (Incorrect)
    Blanket disclosure can impose unnecessary confidentiality costs.
    B. Drop capacity from the contract even though delivery depends on it. (Incorrect)
    Omitting a consequential issue does not resolve it.
    C. Agree on limited evidence or independent verification of the capacity claim. (Correct)
    Staged disclosure can verify the relevant claim without exposing everything.
    D. Accept the capacity claim because the supplier sounds confident. (Incorrect)
    Confidence does not verify the claim.
    E. Publish the claim as fact before obtaining verification. (Incorrect)
    Repetition is not evidence of capacity.

    Review: Chapter 35 · Chapter 38. Back to question 2

  3. Question 3: A

    A. €90 through €100 (Correct)
    The seller's minimum falls to 110−20 = 90, creating overlap.
    B. No ZOPA, because none existed on the original terms (Incorrect)
    Changing costs can create a feasible agreement.
    C. Exactly €105 (Incorrect)
    Splitting the original gap does not respect the buyer's limit.
    D. €110 through €120 (Incorrect)
    This moves the seller's minimum in the wrong direction.
    E. €100 through €110 (Incorrect)
    The buyer still will not pay above €100.

    Review: Chapter 36 · Chapter 37. Back to question 3

  4. Question 4: E

    A. Use the total reduction from the opening demand as the principal measure of the deal's value. (Incorrect)
    A favorable-looking movement can reflect an inflated opening anchor rather than an attractive final package.
    B. Estimate the seller's reservation price by taking the latest offer as the lower bound. (Incorrect)
    The current offer can remain above the seller's actual minimum, so it is not a lower bound on that minimum.
    C. Increase the assessed value of the buyer's BATNA in proportion to the seller's slowing movement. (Incorrect)
    An offer sequence does not itself improve what the buyer can obtain outside the negotiation.
    D. Treat the shrinking increments as evidence that the final package creates the largest joint surplus. (Incorrect)
    Movement in price does not establish that other terms leave no joint gains available.
    E. Treat it as a possible firmness signal while evaluating the offer against outside alternatives. (Correct)
    A concession pattern can communicate firmness without revealing the seller's true limit; the buyer still needs an independent basis for evaluating the package.

    Review: Chapter 36 · Chapter 37. Back to question 4

  5. Question 5: C

    A. A rule forcing both to state the same forecast before negotiating. (Incorrect)
    Agreement need not require identical beliefs.
    B. Ignoring the sales issue because forecasts differ. (Incorrect)
    Difference can be a source of a workable contingent term.
    C. A contingent payment each can evaluate using its own forecast. (Correct)
    Different beliefs may support an outcome-dependent agreement.
    D. An unverified promise that the optimistic forecast will occur. (Incorrect)
    A promise does not resolve uncertainty or verification.
    E. A payment trigger selected after sales are observed. (Incorrect)
    Post-outcome selection creates a manipulable contract.

    Review: Chapter 37 · Chapter 38. Back to question 5

  6. Question 6: E

    A. A guarantee that the chosen package is Pareto efficient (Incorrect)
    Efficiency requires considering the feasible alternatives and both sides' values.
    B. The counterpart's exact reservation price regardless of package design (Incorrect)
    Package preference does not uniquely reveal a full acceptance boundary.
    C. The absence of any distributive conflict (Incorrect)
    Equal value to one side does not erase allocation issues.
    D. The final outcome of any future contingency (Incorrect)
    Preference is not a realized forecast.
    E. Relative priorities across issues (Correct)
    Choosing among packages helps identify which combinations matter to the counterpart.

    Review: Chapter 35 · Chapter 37 · Chapter 38. Back to question 6

  7. Question 7: D

    A. Require an independently verified timestamp for every order entered before month-end. (Incorrect)
    Accurate entry times do not establish whether orders remain valid after cancellations.
    B. Ask salespeople to explain large deviations from their initial monthly forecasts. (Incorrect)
    Forecast explanations do not change which orders earn the bonus.
    C. Increase the bonus rate for orders entered in the final week of the month. (Incorrect)
    This strengthens the incentive to bring orders inside the reporting window without addressing cancellations.
    D. Settle the bonus using orders still valid after the cancellation window. (Correct)
    This excludes orders cancelled within the agreed window from the bonus measure.
    E. Compare each salesperson's recorded orders with the team's monthly average. (Incorrect)
    Relative performance on the same flawed measure leaves the cancellation problem intact.

    Review: Chapter 36 · Chapter 38. Back to question 7

  8. Question 8: D

    A. Require every improved term to be accepted separately before considering the next issue. (Incorrect)
    Separate acceptance can prevent the package trades that make improvements mutually beneficial.
    B. Commit both parties to the midpoint between their next two package proposals. (Incorrect)
    A midpoint need not improve both parties' positions relative to the existing deal.
    C. Suspend the signed agreement until the exploratory negotiation is complete. (Incorrect)
    Suspension exposes the parties to losing the acceptable deal if the search fails.
    D. Retain the signed agreement unless both accept a replacement package. (Correct)
    The existing deal provides a secure fallback while the parties investigate mutually preferred changes.
    E. Allow either party to replace the agreement when estimated total value rises. (Incorrect)
    Higher total value can coexist with one party being worse off, so unilateral replacement is not protective.

    Review: Chapter 37 · Chapter 38. Back to question 8

  9. Question 9: C

    A. €350 through €500 (Incorrect)
    This omits the seller's outside payoff and buyer's transport cost.
    B. €410 through €470 (Incorrect)
    This wrongly deducts the buyer's transport cost from the seller's required revenue.
    C. €440 through €470 (Correct)
    Seller needs p−350 ≥ 90; buyer needs p+30 ≤ 500.
    D. No range, because €500 is above €350 (Incorrect)
    Different cost figures do not themselves imply conflict.
    E. €440 through €500 (Incorrect)
    The buyer's transport cost lowers its maximum price to €470.

    Review: Chapter 36 · Chapter 37. Back to question 9

  10. Question 10: C

    A. No: differing beliefs make a mutually acceptable contingent deal impossible. (Incorrect)
    The specified expectations satisfy both acceptance conditions.
    B. No: buyer gains €20; seller expects only €90. (Incorrect)
    €90 uses the buyer's belief instead of the seller's.
    C. Yes: buyer's expected surplus is €20; seller's expected revenue is €114. (Correct)
    Buyer earns 20 in either state; seller expects 0.8×130 + 0.2×50 = 114.
    D. No: buyer's expected surplus is −€4; seller expects €114. (Incorrect)
    This incorrectly uses the seller's belief for the buyer's payment while retaining the buyer's value expectation.
    E. Yes: buyer gains €60; seller expects €130. (Incorrect)
    €60 uses the high-state asset value instead of expected value; €130 treats the high-state payment as certain.

    Review: Chapter 37 · Chapter 38. Back to question 10

Part IV Review

These ten questions connect ideas from Chapters 23–29. Use the assumptions given in each question and choose the one best answer. Work through the complete set before checking the explanations.

Practice questions

Choose one answer for each of the 10 questions. Complete the set, then select Check answers to see your score and explanations. Each question is worth one point.

1. Before contracting, buyers offer a price based on average quality, causing high-quality sellers with privately known quality to withdraw. After signing and receiving payment, a seller can reduce unobserved effort, lowering the buyer's service quality. Which pair identifies the two information problems?

2. Two teams cooperate internally but resist sharing resources across teams. Which intervention most directly targets the narrow boundary of cooperation?

3. Team members have unique evidence but fear that public disagreement will look disloyal. Which combined procedure most directly addresses both problems?

4. Investors buy after observing earlier purchases, although their private signals favor selling. Which additional evidence would most help distinguish an information cascade from independently justified optimism?

5. Two partners want continued cooperation, but accidental failures sometimes look like deliberate defection. Which rule best addresses this specific problem?

6. A community introduces a small payment for an activity previously treated as a shared duty. Participation falls. Which follow-up best examines the proposed change in social meaning?

7. A trader believes a share is overpriced but expects enthusiastic buyers to push it higher before any correction. Why can waiting be strategically sensible?

8. Employees publicly praise a policy. Anonymous surveys show most oppose it, expect colleagues to oppose it too, and fear the manager's retaliation. Which diagnosis best fits this evidence?

9. In a symmetric coordination game, X pays 10 against X and 0 against Y; Y pays 7 against either action. Players maximize expected monetary payoff. Define risk dominance by the larger interval of beliefs making an action optimal. Which comparison is correct?

10. Researchers want to test whether greater cash holdings increase bubbles in a laboratory market, where traders affect each other's prices. Which randomization best isolates the market-level cash effect?

0 of 10 answered

Answers and explanations

  1. Question 1: A

    A. Adverse selection; moral hazard (Correct)
    The first concerns hidden pre-contract characteristics; the second hidden post-contract action.
    B. Coordination failure; payoff dominance (Incorrect)
    These terms do not identify the hidden-quality and hidden-effort problems.
    C. Reciprocity; conditional cooperation (Incorrect)
    These preference patterns are distinct from the information structure.
    D. Moral hazard; adverse selection (Incorrect)
    This reverses the timing and objects of private information.
    E. Risk dominance; common knowledge (Incorrect)
    Neither private fact is common knowledge, and no equilibrium selection is described.

    Review: Chapter 23 · Chapter 27. Back to question 1

  2. Question 2: D

    A. A ranking rewarding only superiority over the other team (Incorrect)
    This strengthens intergroup competition.
    B. Separate tasks with no need for interteam contact (Incorrect)
    Separation provides no shared cooperative purpose.
    C. A reminder that all members of each team think alike (Incorrect)
    This reinforces an unsupported group stereotype.
    D. A shared task requiring contributions from both teams to succeed (Correct)
    A superordinate goal can make cooperation across the boundary relevant.
    E. More resources for the existing within-team competition (Incorrect)
    This does not address the cross-team boundary.

    Review: Chapter 25 · Chapter 29. Back to question 2

  3. Question 3: A

    A. Collect independent evidence privately, then discuss it under explicit protection for dissent. (Correct)
    This surfaces unshared facts while reducing the identity and social cost of challenge.
    B. Record only the final unanimous statement. (Incorrect)
    A consensus record can hide both missing facts and inhibited doubts.
    C. Ask members to guess which answer their colleagues will approve. (Incorrect)
    This redirects attention toward anticipated social acceptance.
    D. Ask the leader to announce the preferred answer before collecting facts. (Incorrect)
    This increases pressure before independent information is elicited.
    E. Replace factual discussion with a public loyalty vote. (Incorrect)
    That conflates allegiance with evidence.

    Review: Chapter 26 · Chapter 28 · Chapter 29. Back to question 3

  4. Question 4: B

    A. The fact that several investors buy at similar times. (Incorrect)
    Correlated choices can also reflect common independent evidence.
    B. Check whether choices become insensitive to private signals. (Correct)
    A cascade involves following actions despite private information.
    C. A list of the investors' favorite company names. (Incorrect)
    Liking is not evidence about use of private signals.
    D. A claim that every buyer earned a later profit. (Incorrect)
    Later outcomes do not establish how the original decisions used information.
    E. A price chart showing that the price increased. (Incorrect)
    A price increase alone does not identify the mechanism.

    Review: Chapter 26 · Chapter 27. Back to question 4

  5. Question 5: B

    A. Judge intentions solely from the worst observed outcome. (Incorrect)
    Outcome severity alone does not distinguish accident from defection.
    B. Check evidence and allow repair before escalating punishment. (Correct)
    A repair process can preserve cooperation when noisy actions are misread.
    C. Remove every consequence for deliberate exploitation. (Incorrect)
    This weakens deterrence without specifically distinguishing mistakes.
    D. Treat every mistake as grounds for permanent retaliation. (Incorrect)
    Noise can then trigger destructive escalation.
    E. Stop recording the actions that caused disputes. (Incorrect)
    Missing records make diagnosis and repair harder.

    Review: Chapter 23 · Chapter 25. Back to question 5

  6. Question 6: D

    A. Treat the participation decline as proof of lower physical ability. (Incorrect)
    Ability is not the stated mechanism.
    B. Compare only the income of people who continue participating. (Incorrect)
    Post-treatment selection omits those whose participation changed.
    C. Infer that money reduces motivation in every setting. (Incorrect)
    One pattern does not support that generalization.
    D. Randomize payments; compare reported obligation and subsequent participation. (Correct)
    With the task, eligibility, and period fixed, this tests parallel changes in meaning and behavior; it does not identify obligation as the causal mediator.
    E. Increase payment while changing the activity and eligibility rules too. (Incorrect)
    Multiple changes make interpretation difficult.

    Review: Chapter 25 · Chapter 29. Back to question 6

  7. Question 7: B

    A. A later correction guarantees that a short position survives all interim losses. (Incorrect)
    Funding and margin constraints can force early closure.
    B. The path of others' demand affects the risk and timing of a corrective position. (Correct)
    Fundamental disagreement does not eliminate interim losses or funding constraints.
    C. Believing in overpricing removes the need to forecast other traders. (Incorrect)
    Interim prices still depend on others' actions.
    D. Expected short-run demand determines the share's fundamental value by definition. (Incorrect)
    Trading demand and discounted cash-flow value are distinct.
    E. Private valuation is equivalent to a publicly enforceable price cap. (Incorrect)
    A belief does not impose market prices.

    Review: Chapter 23 · Chapter 27. Back to question 7

  8. Question 8: E

    A. A hidden-profile problem caused only by unshared technical facts (Incorrect)
    The stated barrier is retaliation, not absent factual pooling.
    B. Pluralistic ignorance about colleagues' approval (Incorrect)
    Employees correctly expect colleagues to oppose the policy.
    C. Informational updating toward the manager's superior evidence (Incorrect)
    The surveys retain private opposition and specify fear.
    D. Independent private endorsement of the policy (Incorrect)
    The anonymous responses show opposition.
    E. Public compliance under anticipated sanctions (Correct)
    Private attitudes and beliefs about peers agree; fear explains public praise.

    Review: Chapter 26 · Chapter 28 · Chapter 29. Back to question 8

  9. Question 9: D

    A. YY is the only Nash equilibrium. (Incorrect)
    Against X, choosing X gives 10 rather than 7.
    B. XX is both payoff dominant and risk dominant. (Incorrect)
    The higher coordinated payoff does not make X optimal for most beliefs.
    C. YY is payoff dominant; XX is risk dominant. (Incorrect)
    This reverses both comparisons.
    D. XX is payoff dominant; YY is risk dominant. (Correct)
    XX pays 10 each, but X is optimal only when P(X) ≥ 0.7, a smaller interval.
    E. Both equilibria tie on risk dominance. (Incorrect)
    Their optimal-belief intervals have lengths 0.3 and 0.7.

    Review: Chapter 23 · Chapter 24. Back to question 9

  10. Question 10: C

    A. Give experienced markets less cash and novice markets more. (Incorrect)
    Experience is then confounded with cash.
    B. Increase cash and expected dividends together. (Incorrect)
    Both demand capacity and the valuation benchmark change.
    C. Randomize multiple independent markets per cash level, holding asset supply, dividends, and rules fixed. (Correct)
    Treatment applies to the interacting market, with comparable valuation benchmarks.
    D. Randomize half the traders within each market and compare their purchase prices. (Incorrect)
    Traders share prices and influence one another; this does not isolate separate market regimes.
    E. Compare only markets selected because prices rose strongly. (Incorrect)
    Selecting on the outcome biases the treatment comparison.

    Review: Chapter 25 · Chapter 27. Back to question 10

Part VIII. Habits and Behavior Design

When the plan is not the decision-maker: redesign cues, friction, environments, and learning systems.

NoteWhy this part comes last

Knowledge is not implementation. Repetition can move action from deliberation to cue-guided control; craving can outlast liking; and environments can make one action easy while making another nearly invisible. The book ends by turning its science into design.

Guiding questions

  • What cue starts the loop, and what local problem does the response solve?
  • How can an urge be observed without becoming an instruction?
  • What ability, motivation, prompt, friction, or default should change?
  • How can the decision system preserve independence, learn, and remain accountable?

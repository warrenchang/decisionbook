# Part VI question bank

## C35.01 | positions-and-interests | easy | apply | 35
A tenant says, “The rent must be €900.” She wants predictable housing costs while studying. Which is the position, and which is the interest?
+ The €900 demand is the position; predictable costs are the interest. | A position states a proposed term; an interest explains the concern behind it.
- Predictable costs are the position; the €900 demand is the interest. | This reverses the distinction.
- Both are BATNAs because they concern alternatives. | Neither states what happens without agreement.
- Both are objective standards for a fair rent. | A demand and a concern are not independent market standards.
- The demand is an interest only if the landlord accepts it. | Acceptance does not determine whether a term is a position or interest.

## C35.02 | creation-and-claiming | easy | understand | 35
Which action primarily creates joint value rather than allocates an existing surplus?
+ Find a delivery schedule that reduces both parties' operating costs. | The package increases the total benefit available.
- Seek a larger share of a fixed monetary surplus. | This is value claiming.
- Move a price toward one's own reservation value. | This redistributes a given bargaining surplus.
- Reject the other side's first price without exploring issues. | Rejection alone does not create value.
- Conceal the maximum price one is prepared to pay. | Protecting a limit concerns distributive strategy.

## C35.03 | interests-not-positions | medium | apply | 35
Two departments demand the same room every Friday. What question most directly shifts the discussion from positions to interests?
+ What activities need the room, and which timing features matter? | This can reveal needs that alternative schedules could satisfy.
- Which department stated its demand first? | Priority of demand does not reveal the underlying need.
- Which manager can insist more forcefully? | Bargaining pressure does not uncover interests.
- Can both demands be repeated in clearer language? | Clearer positions can leave the substantive conflict unchanged.
- What is the midpoint between the two identical demands? | An arithmetic compromise cannot resolve identical exclusive claims.

## C35.04 | authority | medium | apply | 35
Two negotiators agree on price, delivery, and service terms. Before announcing a completed agreement, which check most directly addresses whether they can commit their organizations?
+ Establish who can approve the package and which approvals remain outstanding. | Agreement between negotiators is not sufficient if authorization or ratification is still required.
- Compare the package with each organization's best alternative to agreement. | This checks acceptability, not the authority to bind the organizations.
- Confirm the milestones by which implementation performance will be reviewed. | Implementation planning does not establish decision authority.
- Record which concessions each side made during the final bargaining round. | Concession history does not show whether the negotiators can authorize the deal.
- Agree on a joint explanation of the benefits for the two organizations. | A persuasive account of benefits does not replace approval.

## C35.05 | joint-decision-map | hard | analyze | 35
A buyer focuses on unit price while the supplier says the proposed delivery pattern causes expensive overtime. Which next move best combines learning with protection of the buyer's interests?
+ Ask which delivery changes reduce overtime and compare complete costed packages. | This explores joint gains without conceding the buyer's limits in advance.
- Accept the current price immediately to show trust. | This concedes before evaluating whether a better package exists.
- Repeat the target price without discussing delivery. | This ignores a potential source of joint value.
- Disclose the buyer's absolute maximum before asking any questions. | That may weaken claiming without resolving the supplier's cost issue.
- Split the price difference while leaving delivery unchanged. | A midpoint can miss the avoidable cost driving the dispute.

## C36.01 | batna | easy | understand | 36
What is a negotiator's BATNA?
+ The best available action if this negotiation fails | It is the relevant outside alternative.
- The most ambitious price to request during bargaining | That is a target or opening aspiration.
- The first offer made by the other party | An offer is part of this negotiation, not the outside alternative.
- The midpoint between the parties' opening offers | A midpoint need not reflect either outside option.
- The minimum gain the other side is willing to accept | That concerns the counterpart's reservation value, not one's own BATNA.

## C36.02 | zopa | easy | apply | 36
A buyer will pay at most €120 and a seller will accept at least €90. What is the price range of possible agreement?
+ €90 through €120 | Both parties can accept a price in that interval.
- €0 through €90 | Prices below €90 violate the seller's limit.
- €120 through €210 | Prices above €120 violate the buyer's limit.
- Exactly €105 | The midpoint is one feasible price, not the whole zone.
- No feasible price | The limits overlap by €30.

## C36.03 | anchor-response | medium | apply | 36
A seller opens far above comparable market prices. Which response best protects the buyer from simply adjusting around that anchor?
+ Revisit independent comparables and justify a counteroffer from them. | An independent basis makes the arbitrary starting point less decisive.
- Split the difference between the opening and an arbitrary low number. | Both numbers may be detached from relevant evidence.
- Assume the opening reveals the seller's reservation value exactly. | An opening offer is not a reliable statement of the minimum.
- Accept the anchor to preserve a cooperative tone. | Cooperation need not require accepting an unsupported price.
- Focus only on the size of the seller's next concession. | A large concession can still leave a poor final price.

## C36.04 | concessions | medium | apply | 36
A buyer can pay more if the seller includes installation. Which offer makes the concession conditional and informative?
+ “If installation is included, I can increase the price by €200.” | The concession is tied to a specific reciprocal term.
- “I will increase the price by €200; we can discuss installation later.” | This concedes before securing the linked term.
- “My opening price was not serious.” | This weakens credibility without defining a trade.
- “I can keep increasing the price until you accept.” | This reveals no limit or reciprocal condition.
- “Installation matters, but I will not discuss it.” | Withholding the issue prevents the proposed exchange.

## C36.05 | unequal-alternatives | hard | apply | 36
A buyer's best alternative costs €1,000 including delivery. The current seller charges a price p plus €80 delivery and a certain €40 adaptation cost. All other benefits are identical. What is the buyer's maximum acceptable p?
+ €880 | p + 80 + 40 must not exceed €1,000.
- €1,000 | This ignores the current offer's extra costs.
- €920 | This includes delivery but omits adaptation.
- €960 | This includes adaptation but omits delivery.
- €1,120 | This adds the extra costs instead of subtracting them from the limit.

## C37.01 | logrolling | easy | apply | 37
A buyer cares most about early delivery; a seller cares most about a longer contract. They exchange early delivery for a longer term. Which technique is illustrated?
+ Logrolling | Parties trade across issues they value differently.
- Splitting each issue exactly in half | The agreement uses differing priorities rather than equal compromises.
- A unilateral concession on every issue | Each party receives something it particularly values.
- A change in the BATNA's definition | The agreement changes contract terms, not the definition of an outside alternative.
- A random allocation of disputed benefits | The trade is deliberately structured around priorities.

## C37.02 | pareto-improvement | easy | understand | 37
Relative to an existing package, what defines a Pareto improvement?
+ Someone is better off and nobody is worse off. | The comparison concerns the parties' preferences over the two packages.
- The total monetary payoff rises even if one party loses. | Higher total value alone does not ensure a Pareto improvement.
- Both parties receive exactly equal payoffs. | Equality is not required.
- The stronger negotiator receives a larger share. | Bargaining strength is not the criterion.
- Every issue is settled at its arithmetic midpoint. | Midpoints need not improve either party's outcome.

## C37.03 | fixed-pie-assumption | medium | analyze | 37
A negotiator assumes that any term benefiting the supplier must cost the buyer equally, before examining their priorities. What opportunity can this assumption hide?
+ Differences in priorities that permit mutually beneficial trades | Some terms can be cheap for one party and valuable to the other.
- The need to compare an agreement with a BATNA | Integrative bargaining still requires outside-option discipline.
- The mathematical identity of all feasible packages | The packages need not be identical.
- The fact that the other party has no interests | The problem concerns mistaken assumptions about those interests.
- A guarantee that every additional issue improves the agreement | Extra issues create opportunities only when usable differences exist.

## C37.04 | priority-map | medium | apply | 37
Which preparation information most directly identifies trades across issues that could create joint value?
+ Both parties' cross-issue priorities, constraints, and costs | Differing priorities reveal exchanges valuable to one side and relatively cheap to the other.
- Market prices for otherwise comparable single-issue contracts | Comparables support standards but may omit cross-issue trade-offs.
- The opening price demands made by each party | Positions need not reveal underlying relative priorities.
- A ranking of each party's outside alternatives | BATNAs protect acceptance decisions but alone do not map issue trades.
- A record of the sequence of previous monetary concessions | Concession history does not directly specify cross-issue preferences.

## C37.05 | package-dominance | hard | apply | 37
Feasible packages give utility pairs (buyer, seller): A=(6,6), B=(8,7), C=(9,5), D=(5,9). Which package is Pareto dominated within this set?
+ A only | B raises both parties' utility relative to A; the remaining packages trade off their interests.
- B only | No listed package gives both parties at least B's utility and one more.
- C only | C gives the buyer the highest listed utility.
- D only | D gives the seller the highest listed utility.
- A and B | B is not dominated by C or D because each lowers one party's utility.

## C38.01 | contingent-contract | easy | apply | 38
A buyer and seller disagree about future demand and make part of the payment depend on verified sales next year. What kind of term is this?
+ A contingent contract term | Payment depends on a specified future outcome.
- A fixed unconditional transfer | The payment varies with the sales outcome.
- A retrospective sunk cost | The payment concerns future performance.
- A private unrecorded aspiration | The condition is part of an agreed term.
- A random first-offer anchor | The term is tied to a verifiable contingency.

## C38.02 | mediation-and-arbitration | easy | understand | 38
Which distinction between mediation and binding arbitration is correct?
+ A mediator helps parties reach agreement; an arbitrator can impose a decision. | The third party's decision authority differs.
- A mediator imposes terms; an arbitrator only helps discussion. | This reverses their usual roles.
- Both require the parties to accept only their original positions. | Either process can consider alternatives.
- Neither process can involve a neutral third party. | Both are forms of third-party assistance.
- Arbitration is simply another name for private negotiation. | Binding arbitration delegates a decision to the arbitrator.

## C38.03 | verifiable-contingency | medium | apply | 38
The parties want a bonus tied to real on-time deliveries. Which measurement design best supports that goal?
+ Agreed delivery records, independent checks, and a dispute procedure | The metric fits the goal and can be verified and challenged.
- Audited total revenue without any delivery-time information | Auditability does not repair a metric unrelated to timeliness.
- The seller's self-reported delivery rate without access to records | A relevant measure still needs credible verification.
- Agreed delivery records with no rule for conflicting timestamps | A likely measurement dispute remains unresolved.
- The number of dispatch notices, regardless of actual arrival | This can reward an action that misses the agreed outcome.

## C38.04 | minimum-agreement | medium | apply | 38
A signed agreement says only, “The teams will cooperate to improve delivery.” Which addition most fully makes implementation testable?
+ Owners, measurable obligations, deadlines, and review and failure rules | These identify who does what and how completion and problems are handled.
- A delivery target without assigning responsibility | The target leaves ownership unresolved.
- Named owners without defining acceptable delivery performance | Responsibility alone does not specify completion criteria.
- Monthly review meetings without measures or failure procedures | A meeting schedule does not define performance or consequences.
- A general escalation clause without task deadlines | Dispute handling alone does not specify the underlying obligations.

## C38.05 | contingent-valuation | hard | apply | 38
A seller chooses a fixed €100 payment or €160 if demand is high and €40 if low. The seller assigns high demand probability 0.6, values money linearly, and ignores discounting. Which option is preferred?
+ The contingent payment, with expected value €112 | 0.6×160 + 0.4×40 = 112, above 100.
- The fixed payment, because the contingent value is €88 | This reverses the high- and low-state probabilities.
- The fixed payment, because the contingent value is €96 | This omits the low-state payment.
- The options tie at €100 | They would tie at probability 0.5, not 0.6.
- The contingent payment, because it guarantees €160 | The favorable payment is conditional rather than guaranteed.

## P06.01 | interest-versus-alternative | easy | apply | 35,36
A job seeker wants more time with family. Her best available option if negotiations fail is keeping her current job. Which pair identifies these two facts?
+ An interest and a BATNA | Family time is an underlying concern; staying is an outside course of action.
- A BATNA and an interest | This reverses the roles.
- An opening offer and a target | Neither fact specifies a proposed negotiated term.
- A reservation value and a concession | No acceptance limit or change in an offer is supplied.

## P06.02 | staged-verification | easy | apply | 35,38
A supplier claims spare capacity but says its full customer list is confidential. Which approach best checks capacity while protecting unrelated sensitive information?
+ Agree on limited evidence or independent verification of the capacity claim. | Staged disclosure can verify the relevant claim without exposing everything.
- Require the complete customer database before discussing relevance. | Blanket disclosure can impose unnecessary confidentiality costs.
- Accept the capacity claim because the supplier sounds confident. | Confidence does not verify the claim.
- Drop capacity from the contract even though delivery depends on it. | Omitting a consequential issue does not resolve it.

## P06.03 | no-price-zopa | medium | apply | 36,37
On existing terms a seller needs at least €110 and a buyer will pay at most €100. A changed delivery method saves the seller €20 without costing the buyer anything. What is the new price ZOPA if the seller passes the saving into its minimum?
+ €90 through €100 | The seller's minimum falls to 110−20 = 90, creating overlap.
- €100 through €110 | The buyer still will not pay above €100.
- Exactly €105 | Splitting the original gap does not respect the buyer's limit.
- €110 through €120 | This moves the seller's minimum in the wrong direction.
- No ZOPA, because none existed on the original terms | Changing costs can create a feasible agreement.

## P06.04 | concession-and-reservation | medium | analyze | 36,37
A seller makes progressively smaller price concessions on an unchanged package. Which use of this information is most defensible for the buyer?
+ Treat it as a possible firmness signal while evaluating the offer against outside alternatives. | A concession pattern can communicate firmness without revealing the seller's true limit; the buyer still needs an independent basis for evaluating the package.
- Estimate the seller's reservation price by taking the latest offer as the lower bound. | The current offer can remain above the seller's actual minimum, so it is not a lower bound on that minimum.
- Treat the shrinking increments as evidence that the final package creates the largest joint surplus. | Movement in price does not establish that other terms leave no joint gains available.
- Use the total reduction from the opening demand as the principal measure of the deal's value. | A favorable-looking movement can reflect an inflated opening anchor rather than an attractive final package.
- Increase the assessed value of the buyer's BATNA in proportion to the seller's slowing movement. | An offer sequence does not itself improve what the buyer can obtain outside the negotiation.

## P06.05 | forecast-disagreement | medium | analyze | 37,38
Two parties disagree about a future sales forecast but agree on how sales can be verified. Which possibility should they examine?
+ A contingent payment each can evaluate using its own forecast. | Different beliefs may support an outcome-dependent agreement.

## P06.07 | contingent-incentives | medium | analyze | 36,38
A sales bonus rewards orders entered before month-end, even if customers later cancel them. Which contract revision most directly aligns the bonus with sales retained after cancellations?
+ Settle the bonus using orders still valid after the cancellation window. | This excludes orders cancelled within the agreed window from the bonus measure.

## P06.08 | post-agreement-search | medium | apply | 37,38
After signing an acceptable agreement, two parties want to explore whether they have missed opportunities for mutual gain. Which rule best protects both parties during that search?
+ Retain the signed agreement unless both accept a replacement package. | The existing deal provides a secure fallback while the parties investigate mutually preferred changes.
- Suspend the signed agreement until the exploratory negotiation is complete. | Suspension exposes the parties to losing the acceptable deal if the search fails.
- Allow either party to replace the agreement when estimated total value rises. | Higher total value can coexist with one party being worse off, so unilateral replacement is not protective.

## P06.09 | total-cost-and-zopa | hard | apply | 36,37
A buyer's alternative costs €500 all-inclusive. Under the proposed deal the buyer pays price p plus €30 transport. The seller incurs €350 in costs only if this deal proceeds; its best alternative provides €90 net. No other differences matter. What price range makes both at least as well off as their alternatives?
+ €440 through €470 | Seller needs p−350 ≥ 90; buyer needs p+30 ≤ 500.
- €350 through €500 | This omits the seller's outside payoff and buyer's transport cost.
- €410 through €470 | This wrongly deducts the buyer's transport cost from the seller's required revenue.
- €440 through €500 | The buyer's transport cost lowers its maximum price to €470.
- No range, because €500 is above €350 | Different cost figures do not themselves imply conflict.

## P06.10 | contingent-mutual-acceptance | hard | apply | 37,38
A buyer values an asset at €150 if demand is high and €70 if low. It believes high demand has probability 0.5; the seller believes 0.8 and needs expected revenue of at least €100. Both are risk neutral. A proposed payment is €130 if high and €50 if low; the buyer's outside payoff is zero. Is this acceptable to both on these criteria?
+ Yes: buyer's expected surplus is €20; seller's expected revenue is €114. | Buyer earns 20 in either state; seller expects 0.8×130 + 0.2×50 = 114.
- No: buyer's expected surplus is −€4; seller expects €114. | This incorrectly uses the seller's belief for the buyer's payment while retaining the buyer's value expectation.
- No: buyer gains €20; seller expects only €90. | €90 uses the buyer's belief instead of the seller's.
- Yes: buyer gains €60; seller expects €130. | €60 uses the high-state asset value instead of expected value; €130 treats the high-state payment as certain.
- No: differing beliefs make a mutually acceptable contingent deal impossible. | The specified expectations satisfy both acceptance conditions.

## Chapter-by-chapter decisions

- **11 — Anchors, Halos, and Decoys: unchanged.** Main sections distinguish anchoring, halo effects, decoys, evaluation modes, and their practical correction. The subscription and hiring cases already develop those concepts within the sections.
- **12 — Framing: unchanged.** Main sections distinguish what framing changes, equivalent descriptions, assumed choices, memory/causality, and reframing in practice. Personal stories and the predict-first exercise are already note boxes rather than competing conceptual sections.
- **13 — Priming, Fluency, and Familiarity:** moved “The risk of the familiar” directly after the repetition discussion, keeping the familiarity argument together. Integrated “Make accurate information fluent” into “Clarity without borrowed credibility,” so the launch-team redesign follows the fluency evidence rather than appearing as a second main section on the same practical principle. Preserved every word of the recently added scuba-diving, mood-memory, and spouse-argument discussion.
- **14 — Base Rates and Updating:** renamed “Worked update: the same screen in two applicant pools” to “A test result depends on the starting population.” The developed opening example remains intact; its heading now expresses the concept that gives it a place alongside the probability sections.
- **15 — Randomness and Overconfidence:** renamed “Which sequence looks random?” to “Randomness and apparent patterns,” retaining the coin exercise as its demonstration. Promoted “Superstition and the illusion of control” from a subsection of regression to a main section: accidental reinforcement and inflated causal control are related inference problems, not types of regression. Integrated the two-paragraph planning-fallacy application into overconfidence. Demoted and renamed “From a project story to a forecast system” to “Combine causal forecasts with the outside view” within calibration; the project case now develops that forecasting method.
- **16 — Risky Decision Making:** renamed the insurance-specific main section “Match risk exposure to the capacity to bear it,” with “Diversification and shared risks” as its developed subsection. This makes insurance and project portfolios applications of capacity and dependence rather than an unexplained project “extension” of an insurance example. Integrated the one-paragraph “Identification requires more than a choice” subsection into its existing elicitation research lens. Kept the named Allais and Ellsberg sections: they introduce distinct violations/uncertainty concepts rather than incidental case studies.
- **17 — Prospect Theory:** renamed “One applied audit across three settings” to “Check reference points and future consequences,” making the organizing decision principle visible. Integrated “A practical ownership test” into the endowment-effect subsection, where the checklist tests the competing explanations just introduced. Retained the research lens's separate endowment, effort, status-quo, and bracketing mechanisms.
- **18 — Decisions From Experience:** renamed the supplier-specific main heading “Audit the experience behind a decision.” The supplier case remains the chapter's developed synthesis of sampling, description, choice, and learning design. Retained the substantial mechanism subsections and the research lens's disaster application.
- **19 — Intertemporal Decision Making:** moved the saving and difficult-conversation examples into the main “Make delayed goals easier to act on now” discussion, before commitment devices. These plans no longer appear subordinate to a mechanism requiring binding restrictions or costs. Removed the two-paragraph conversation subheading and kept its anchor. Retained developed subsections on future-self representations and interventions.
- **20 — Mental Accounting:** reorganized nine overlapping subsections into the four operations promised by the main section: “Label: how categories change value”; “Close: sunk costs, realized outcomes, and targets”; “Couple: when consumption repays a purchase”; and “Bracket: which outcomes belong together?” Integrated budget cases under labeling; house money, break-even, realization, and daily targets under closing; diversification and hedonic editing under bracketing. Adjusted transitions and the parent heading's ordering. No empirical example, numerical result, table, figure, or evidence qualification was removed.
- **21 — Habits, Wanting, and Self-Control: unchanged.** Main sections distinguish habit definition, formation, persistence, reward mechanisms, routine change, and urge/lapse responses. The reward subsections identify distinct processes; BRAIN and replacement-response sections are developed instructions rather than isolated examples. Personal anecdotes and research extensions already have their appropriate box treatment.
- **22 — Deciding for a Better Life:** integrated the short “Turn the evidence into a personal decision” section into the well-being decision audit, where the personal hypothesis now specifies how to test a change. Moved the prosocial-spending experiment into the discussion of relationships and resources, connecting it to how additional income could be used. The sequence now moves from focusing illusion to measurement and then to the audit.

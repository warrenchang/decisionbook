# Section hierarchy review: Chapters 1–10 and front matter

Reviewed the chapter narratives and the configured front matter/part introductions. The editorial question was whether headings identify concepts, mechanisms, or developed practical tasks at a comparable level. Short examples can remain useful without their own heading. Navigational metadata and pedagogical components need not meet a minimum paragraph count.

## Chapters 1–10

- **1 — How Decisions Should Be Made:** integrated the short definitions subsection into the rational-choice benchmark and the six-example summary into the section it summarizes. Kept the six developed, deliberately parallel context examples and every figure/table. Preserved old anchors and added a transition to the comparison table.
- **2 — Building a Better Decision:** unchanged. Alternatives, opportunity cost, information value, robustness, and decision analysis are distinct conceptual tasks; the job-offer examples already develop them within their sections.
- **3 — Attention:** integrated the digital-service application into the attention discussion and the organizational application into attention design. Added transitions identifying those applications. The neural-network research box is no longer accidentally subordinate to a digital-service example.
- **4 — The Predictive Mind:** integrated the hollow-face example into perceptual expectations. Consolidated nine small research-lens subsections into three developed themes: learned structure and current context; learning and adapting to evidence; prompts as framing and priming. Moved image/video generation beside the account of generative procedures. Preserved the separate Shirl Jennings research lens and all studies and illustrations.
- **5 — Expectations:** integrated the short post-deadline illness example into the symptom discussion, with a transition from treatment settings to everyday symptom interpretation. Preserved the study's qualifications.
- **6 — Valuation:** integrated music and culturally valued objects into **Experience and social meaning shape value**, following social approval and affiliation. Moved the music-sales neuroforecasting study into the existing neuroscience research lens, beside common currency, distributed value signals, and reverse inference. Integrated the waiting-cost illustration into **Motives change the decision landscape**. Kept price as cost/signal, emotion, self-relevance, and motives as distinct concepts. An independent editorial reviewer checked the new organization; the neural lens was then reordered so its account of distributed signals stays together.
- **7 — The Narrator After Choice:** renamed the dissonance section **How actions reshape attitudes** to cover both dissonance and self-perception. Integrated the sole child heading. Combined the practical journal and rationalization-detector subsections into a continuous sequence: record before choice, review against that record, test competing explanations, and distinguish process from outcome.
- **8 — Intuition and Deliberation:** integrated three two-paragraph subsections within the AI-reasoning research lens into a continuous explanation. Kept the chapter's distinct conceptual sections, including metacognition and emotion. Scientific comparisons and limitations are unchanged.
- **9 — Availability, Affect, and Resemblance:** unchanged in this task. The distinct heuristic mechanisms warrant separate sections. Preserved the previous turn's uncommitted associative-memory addition.
- **10 — Beliefs That Defend Themselves:** unchanged. The sections distinguish confirmation, motivation, overconfidence in explanation, belief perseverance, and correction; examples already serve those mechanisms.

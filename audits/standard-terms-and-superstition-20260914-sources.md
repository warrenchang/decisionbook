# Terminology and superstition: source verification

Verified 14 September 2026. Source verification was independently reviewed. Lecture materials identify coverage and examples; they are not cited as published evidence.

| Claim | Published source | Verification and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Type 1/2 and System 1/2 are both established labels | Evans & Stanovich (2013), *Perspectives on Psychological Science, 8*, 223–241. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691612460685) | Full text, pp. 224–226 and Tables 1–2 inspected. Authors prefer Type to avoid implying exactly two systems. Chapter 8 uses System for familiarity while explaining the process-family interpretation. Speed and accuracy do not perfectly separate the families. |
| Representativeness is the conventional heuristic name | Kahneman & Tversky (1972), *Cognitive Psychology, 3*, 430–454. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0285(72)90016-3); Tversky & Kahneman (1974), *Science, 185*, 1124–1131. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.185.4157.1124) | Similarity or resemblance explains the operation; it does not replace the technical label. Existing references retained. |
| Three forms of overconfidence | Moore & Healy (2008), *Psychological Review, 115*, 502–517. [Author paper](https://healy.econ.ohio-state.edu/papers/Moore_Healy-TroubleWithOverconfidence.pdf) | Overestimation, overplacement, and overprecision are coordinate forms. Planning fallacy is a forecast problem/application that may involve optimistic estimates and overprecision. |
| Six of eight pigeons developed recognizable repeated movements under response-independent food delivery | Skinner (1948), *Journal of Experimental Psychology, 38*, 168–172. [Original scan](https://gwern.net/doc/statistics/causality/1948-skinner.pdf); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0055873) | Original p. 168 verified. Circling and head movements documented. Accidental reinforcement is attributed to Skinner as his interpretation; no claim that the birds’ beliefs were measured. |
| Later behavior was organized around food timing | Staddon & Simmelhag (1971), *Psychological Review, 78*, 3–43. [Institutional metadata](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/659947); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0030305) | Metadata and original indexed abstract verified; full article access unresolved. No detailed experimental estimates are reported from this source. The chapter confines the claim to timing/organization and the challenge to simple accidental reinforcement. |
| Feeding patterns and the feeding environment help explain repeated behavior | Timberlake & Lucas (1985), *Journal of the Experimental Analysis of Behavior, 44*, 279–299. [Primary full text](https://bpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/about.illinoisstate.edu/dist/6/45/files/2019/10/timberlake-and-lucas-1985.pdf); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1901/jeab.1985.44-279) | Primary experiments support species-typical feeding patterns and effects of the feeding arrangement. Their account is distinguished from the earlier timing account. |
| LeBron James performs a chalk toss | NBA (18 May 2009), *LeBron’s chalk toss*. [Official video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yY2dUuMj8q8) | Verified behavioral illustration. It does not establish a private belief that chalk causes victory or that the ritual improves performance. |
| Tiger Woods recounts wins in red and unsuccessful tournaments in blue | PGA TOUR (30 April 2024), *Tiger Woods appears on “The Tonight Show Starring Jimmy Fallon” to discuss Sun Day Red and more*. [Official report](https://www.pgatour.com/article/news/latest/2024/04/30/tiger-woods-goes-on-the-tonight-show-jimmy-fallon-sun-day-red) | Direct Woods remarks reproduced by the official tour, in a humorous interview. Different tournaments do not constitute a controlled color experiment. No diagnosis of his beliefs is inferred. |

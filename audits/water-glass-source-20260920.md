# Water-glass placement: source verification

**Superseded:** Removed from Chapter 39 and the book references at the user's request on 2026-09-20 because the anecdote does not provide direct evidence. The integration and verification entries below document the earlier revision, not the current book.

User-supplied claim: half a glass of water on the left of a desk is the best way to establish water drinking, adding about four glasses per day compared with the right.

## Identified sources

- Jian Shuo Wang, March 16, 2009, *A Glass Cup of Water on Left Hand*: https://home.wangjianshuo.com/20090316_a_glass_cup_of_water_on_left_hand.htm. Personal account of a glass kept about 60% full on the left, followed by more drinking. This version does not supply the left/right daily counts.
- Jian Shuo Wang, July 2, 2021, *喂养青龙的、左手边六分满的玻璃杯*: https://home.wangjianshuo.com/cn/20210702_喂养青龙的、左手边六分满的玻璃杯.htm. The author labels this a repost of an older article. Reports eight to ten glasses per day with the glass on the left, about four on the right, and often none when behind him. Explicitly describes proposed explanations as conjectures not tested experimentally.
- Lally et al. (2010), *How are habits formed: Modelling habit formation in the real world*, https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674. Publisher abstract checked: 96 volunteers chose a daily eating, drinking, or activity behavior in a consistent context for 12 weeks; 82 supplied sufficient data. This formal habit-formation study is already covered in Chapter 21. It does not test left/right placement or half-full glasses.

The Wang accounts are a close match to the supplied story; the exact intermediate source of the user's wording was not located. No controlled study establishing the specific placement/fill-level/daily-gain claim was identified. The reported glass counts are personal recollections without a stated standardized volume, randomized order, comparison sample, or uncertainty estimate; they are not a transferable four-glass effect size. The accounts also do not establish long-term habit formation or health benefits.

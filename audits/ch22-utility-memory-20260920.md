# Chapter 22: utility, memory, and anticipation

The chapter now distinguishes a forecast of enjoyment from value reflected in choice, and uses experienced utility consistently in Figure 22.1 and Table 22.1. The existing figure layout is preserved. The supplied toddler-vacation story appears in a normally expanded personal-experience note box, without an invented response or outcome.

The new discussion separates using past memories to forecast enjoyment from choosing an event for the memory it promises. It distinguishes retrospective evaluation from pleasure or distress during recollection, and explains anticipation as present experience. The no-memory holiday and paid unpleasant episode remain thought experiments, without unsupported claims about how most people respond.

## Source verification

- Kahneman, Wakker, and Sarin (1997), author-hosted paper: decision utility is inferred from choice; predicted utility concerns beliefs about future experienced utility. https://personal.eur.nl/wakker/pdfspubld/97.1kwsqje.pdf
- Benjamin et al. (2012), publisher abstract: hypothetical choice can differ from predicted well-being. https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.102.5.2083
- Wirtz et al. (2003), publisher abstract and metadata: remembered spring-break experience predicted stated desire to repeat it in path analyses. The chapter does not turn this into a causal effect on actual bookings or a population-wide memory weight. https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9280.03455
- Loewenstein (1987), author-hosted paper and publisher metadata: savoring and dread in an account of delayed consumption. https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/AnticipationDelayed.pdf

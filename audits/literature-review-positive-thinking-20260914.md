# Appendix E: positive-thinking review practice

Date: 14 September 2026.

## Revision

Positive thinking is now the main example throughout question development, search, evidence comparison, synthesis, gap identification, and the practice proposal. The exercise includes the author's preferred title, an explicit And–But–Therefore problem statement, the starting question about positive expectations, and steps for refining the question and developing a defensible review plan.

The motivating tension is confidence that supports action versus feeling prepared without adequate assessment or preparation. This is a question to investigate, not a conclusion that confidence causes poor preparation. The text distinguishes expectations of likely success from positive fantasies and shows why the research question must change if it covers both. It treats any new gap as provisional until checked through a wider search.

The existing three savings studies, their comparison table, and their substantive methodological lessons remain in an optional second example. Its motivation now explains how accepting defaults can encourage entry while also shaping subsequent contributions, and why those outcomes need to be connected to additional saving. The existing table identifier is retained. No existing published reference was removed. The inherited phrase attributing the teaching example to a lecture was removed; unpublished slides are not cited.

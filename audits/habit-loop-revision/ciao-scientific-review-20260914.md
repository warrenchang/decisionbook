# CIAO figure: scientific clarification and redesign

The earlier dashed arrow from Outcome to the Impulse–Action connector implied that learning was localized to that transition. The revised figure removes that arrow and uses four equal ring segments, with Learning across repetitions as a central annotation. Three solid arrows trace an episode. Following the author's subsequent request, a dashed Outcome-to-Cue arrow closes the circle and is defined in the caption as a later encounter with the cue, rather than an outcome causing or changing the cue.

CIAO is presented as a teaching mnemonic. Gardner (2015) explicitly proposes that cues generate impulses through learned stimulus–response associations, and that such impulses need not generate behavior. Wood and Neal (2007) describe the learning of context–response associations through repeated performance. These sources support the terms and learning explanation; they do not establish CIAO as a fixed sequence of four obligatory stages.

The chapter now cites Gardner for the meaning of impulse and retains Wood and Neal (2007) and Wood and Rünger (2016) for learning and habit persistence. The existing bedtime example and distinctions between initiating a routine and continuing to scroll were preserved.

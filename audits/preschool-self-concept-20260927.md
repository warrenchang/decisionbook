## Sources checked on 2026-09-27

- **Butler (1998), Child Development, 69(4), 1054–1073.** [Publisher abstract](https://academic.oup.com/chidev/article-abstract/69/4/1054/8284487), also available through the [author's university record](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/age-trends-in-the-use-of-social-and-temporal-comparison-for-self-/). The abstract reports two studies with 840 children aged 4–8. The chapter uses only the abstract-supported finding that four- and five-year-olds rated themselves more favorably after outperforming another child than after doing worse and could explain their judgments in comparative terms. No uninspected task details or effect sizes are supplied. The issue date is 1998; the later electronic publication date does not change the citation year.
- **Asaba and Gweon (2022), PNAS, 119(32), e2105642119.** [Author-hosted full article](https://bingschool.stanford.edu/sites/bingschool/files/media/file/asaba-gweon-2022-young-children-infer-and-manage-what-others-think-about-them_0.pdf), especially pp. 3–4. The passage describes Experiment 2b, with 100 three- and four-year-olds, separately from the broader age range of Experiment 2a. Choice of the familiar toy differed when the observer missed versus witnessed the child's eventual success (53.8% versus 29.2%, reported Fisher exact p=.015). The text describes the between-condition difference; it does not claim an above-chance preference in the former condition. Toy outcomes were remotely controlled. This is evidence about observer-sensitive presentation of competence, not a test of superiority over peers, an innate motive, or a longitudinal identity trajectory. The article reports memory-check exclusions and weaker findings in some later experiments; the chapter does not generalize the selected result to uniform success across all experiments.
- **Cimpian et al. (2007), Psychological Science, 18(4), 314–316.** [Author-hosted full article](https://markmanlab.stanford.edu/publications/Cimpian-Arce-Markman-Dweck-2007.pdf). Twenty-four children, mean age 4 years 6 months, range 4 years 1 month to 5 years 1 month, enacted puppet scenarios with imaginary drawings. Praise described the person or a particular successful performance. After imagined errors, the person-praise condition had a higher composite helplessness score; sadness and stated preference for a different activity also differed individually. The passage says role-play and immediate responses, not actual drawing persistence or durable personality change. It does not claim that all individual measures were statistically significant.

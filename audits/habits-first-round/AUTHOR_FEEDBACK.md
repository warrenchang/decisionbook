# Author feedback: sentence rhythm and explicit explanation

Applied September 10, 2026, after the first independent editorial pass.

## Explicit author guidance

- Do not overuse short sentences. Vary sentence lengths, combining related ideas where doing so improves flow.
- Use the author's revised opening: a student writing a term paper, with social media open on the phone.
- Use the invitation to keep a personal example in mind “as we continue our discussion.”
- Develop the learning process in the example: finding friends' posts can make checking rewarding; repeated checking in the same setting can then make pauses or boredom prompt the action even when an interesting post does not appear.
- Replace “this kind of control.” The referent was ambiguous, and control had not yet been introduced.

## Implementation

The supplied opening and transition were incorporated with light grammatical editing. The reward example describes an illustrative behavioral sequence without asserting a measured change in the fictional student's brain. The definition now explains that repeating an action in a recurring context can make familiar cues prompt it with less need for a fresh decision.

The same sentence-rhythm preference was applied selectively elsewhere in Chapter 21, including the walking example, the account of immediate relief, the change plan, and the closing scene. Short sentences remain where they provide emphasis. Later mentions of the inbox, assignment, and messaging app were aligned with the term-paper/social-media example, and the later claim that no notification occurred was removed to match the revised opening.

The expanded learning example made the first paragraph under “How habits form” repetitive, so that section now moves directly to interpreting the diagram. Figures, numerical results, references, and the distinction between habits, wanting, and liking were preserved.

These comments establish guidance for future book revisions. This pass applies them to Chapter 21; it does not impose a new structure on the other chapters.

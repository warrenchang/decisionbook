## Content and source decisions

- **Chapter 10, positive illusions:** distinguishes encouragement and hope from demonstrably inaccurate beliefs. Restores Taylor and Brown (1988), and adds Colvin and Block’s critique (1994) and Taylor and Brown’s response (1994). Describes an evidence debate rather than treating inaccurate optimism as a universal benefit.
- **Chapter 32, curiosity:** restores Loewenstein’s (1994) information-gap account, using the fictional restaurant pitch to show how a recognizable unanswered question can invite investigation. The example is illustrative, not a study result; attention does not establish the truth of a claim.
- **Chapter 34, forgiveness:** restores Worthington (2006) and Toussaint et al. (2015), distinguishing forgiveness from reconciliation and trust, and explaining the voluntary REACH approach. Adds Ho et al. (2024), a randomized waitlist-controlled trial with 4,598 adults. The note reports the two-week self-report findings without attributing them solely to forgiveness or inferring lasting physical-health benefits.

Four previously removed references are restored verbatim; three additional references support the qualifications and trial discussion. No existing chapter reference is removed. ## Primary sources checked

- Loewenstein (1994), author-hosted original paper: https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/PsychofCuriosity.pdf — information-gap account and the role of perceived knowledge gaps. DOI: 10.1037/0033-2909.116.1.75.
- Taylor and Brown (1988), author-hosted original paper: https://taylorlab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/5/2014/10/1988_Illusion-and-well-being_A-social-psychological-perspective-on-mental-health.pdf — proposed links between positive illusions and well-being.
- Colvin and Block (1994), original abstract and metadata: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8078973/ — critique of the evidence and formulation, not proof of the opposite claim.

- Worthington (2006), publisher metadata: https://www.routledge.com/Forgiveness-and-Reconciliation-Theory-and-Application/Worthington-Jr/p/book/9781583913338 — restored book reference. The entire book was not reread for this edit.
- Worthington’s author explanation of REACH: https://www.evworthington-forgiveness.com/reach-forgiveness-of-others — acronym and practice structure.
- Toussaint et al. (2015), publisher metadata: https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-94-017-9993-5 — scholarly edited volume, not a single causal study. The entire volume was not reread for this edit.
- Ho et al. (2024), full original trial: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11812785/ — outcomes measured two weeks after assignment; randomized immediate versus delayed workbook receipt; follow-up limits described in the paper.

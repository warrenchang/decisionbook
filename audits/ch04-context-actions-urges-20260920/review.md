## Scientific distinctions

- Active inference is presented as a theoretical account in which a generative model represents actions and their consequences; an internal model is not described as an inflexible command.
- Learned context–response associations can prompt a familiar action without a fresh evaluation of consequences (Wood & Neal, 2007). This is not treated as evidence that every habit requires online outcome prediction.
- The proposed attentional/predictive account of increased urgency is identified as an interpretation. Clarkson et al. (2020) supplies evidence of cue-sensitive sensation and physiological responses, not a test establishing that unique mechanism.
- The smoking-availability result (Bailey et al., 2010) grounds the preview of opportunity and craving. Chapter 21 supplies the full design and distinguishes felt urges from readiness to act.

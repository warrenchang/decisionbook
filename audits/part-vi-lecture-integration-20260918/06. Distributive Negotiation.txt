Uninformed first offer: Examples

When director Billy Wilder went to hire detective novelist Raymond
Chandler to write the 1944 classic       Double Indemnity, Chandler was
new to Hollywood. During the negotiation meeting , he bluffly
demanded $150 per week and warned Wilder that it might take him
three weeks to finish the project. However, Wilder planned to pay
Chandler $750 per week and knew that movie scripts took months to
write. Lucky for Chandler, Wilder valued their relationship and called
an agent to represent Chandler in the negotiations.

How to make the first offer? Acquiring information first

• Opening offer should not give away too much of the bargaining zone
• Learn about each other    ’s interests before making offers
  –  Do not rush to make first offers; acquire information first
  –  Late first offers are more likely to lead to creative agreements that meet
     parties’ interests
• Provide justification for your offer

 How to avoid being anchored? Re-anchoring

•  Think about the opponents’ BATNA and reservation value
•  Think about your target point; do not adjust your target accordingly
•  Counteroffer and re-anchor right away
   –  Provide justification for your offer
   –  Do not ask for justification of anchor: discussion makes it more powerful
•  Never accept the first offer

Concessions

• Concessions are almost always necessary to reach an agreement
• Concessions may not have the desired effect if not made properly
  – Salespeople making concession do not improve customer satisfaction

Summary
• Goal of distributive negotiation: push settlement close to the other
  party   ’s reser vation point, capturing most bargaining surplus.
• If the bargaining zone is negative, do not waste time negotiating
• Whether to make the first offer depend on the information both
  parties have
• Range offers and precise offers provide stronger anchoring effect
• Never accept the first offer
• Make a large number of small concessions and gradually decrease
  the size of concessions

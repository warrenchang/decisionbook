## Conclusion

The important BE concept families already have substantive homes in the book. Adding a second survey of them would create repetition. The useful changes are to develop particular studies where an existing explanation has become too abstract and to keep the types of evidence distinct.

Two such changes were implemented within existing sections:

1. **Chapter 25:** the norm-elicitation discussion now illustrates giving/taking and opting out with concrete experiments, instead of referring generically to dictator-game variants. List (2007) and Lazear, Malmendier, and Weber (2012) were added to the local bibliography. The interpretation still distinguishes generosity, obligation, self-image, and perceived expectations.
2. **Chapter 40:** the discussion of physical accessibility now includes Thorndike et al. (2012), corresponding to current BE06 slide 36. The text reports **water's share of beverage purchases**, separates percentage points from percentages, and identifies the sequential, nonrandomized labeling-plus-placement design. It does not claim that water placement alone was isolated or that purchases establish health improvement.

Chapter 26 already owns informational versus normative influence, norms, social proof, cascades, and conformity with relevant studies. Chapter 25 owns cooperation, reciprocity as a social preference, norm enforcement, and group identity. Expanding Chapter 28 to cover interpersonal influence and compliance can therefore work without adding another chapter: its discussion should explain how a request or social situation changes behavior, then refer to those existing mechanisms. Chapter 30 can concentrate on how messages change beliefs and attitudes. The parent review owns the actual Chapter 28/30 restructure.

## Material deliberately not imported as established science

- Repeated administrative slides, experiment websites, participant/payment instructions, assessment dates, instructor biography and course evaluations do not belong in the book's explanatory text.
- Unpublished classroom results and screenshots can inspire an exercise, but cannot substantiate a population claim without a documented design, data, and analysis. Their concepts are already mapped to published evidence or worked examples.
- Multiple versions of the same game, return chart, video scene or historical anecdote are not separate missing theories. A developed example can carry their shared teaching point without reproducing every variant.
- Claims in *Mind Magic* that an autonomic system acts as a gatekeeper to the subconscious or that thought manifests external events should not be imported as established mechanisms. The book already retains the testable attention, expectation, valuation, visualization and well-being material with more specific evidence.
- Generic claims that familiar rewards always lose pleasure, willpower is a fuel tank, every new context erases a habit, or every nudge preserves welfare would contradict the book's qualified treatment. A source-slide wording is not automatically more authoritative than the primary study.
- Theories, formal models, descriptive taxonomies and practical checklists need different standards. A model may be useful because it states assumptions and generates testable predictions; a checklist may organize a decision without having been validated as a complete intervention. Labeling either as such is preferable to inventing supporting experiments.

## What changed in the writing and evidence

Chapter 28 develops the copier, unsolicited favor, successive-request, low-ball, scarcity, and liking experiments through their comparisons and outcomes. It retains authority, groupthink, shared-information bias, and bystander research, with the safety-email case connecting the later sections. Compliance, obedience, conformity, and persuasion are explicitly distinguished.

Chapter 30 follows an organizational proposal through credibility, argument evaluation, self-persuasion, and resistance. Developed studies now include Hovland and Weiss; Petty, Cacioppo, and Goldman; Chaiken; Müller and colleagues; advice giving; moral reframing; vocal tone; and inoculation. The two-sided-message and fear-appeal passages were corrected to match their cited meta-analyses. Model updating, the barrier matrix, and influence checklists are identified as practical syntheses; predictive processing is an analogy, and Aristotle supplies historical vocabulary. These are not presented as independently validated intervention packages.

Additional course-coverage repairs fit within existing sections:

- **Chapter 13:** typicality, novelty, MAYA, and preference changes with exposure; experimental liking results are separated from observational sales evidence.
- **Chapter 25:** giving versus taking, avoiding a sharing opportunity, and cooperative cascades through randomly rematched groups.
- **Chapter 33:** Asymmetric Information Management and its mixed extension, without treating sparse speech as evidence of lying.
- **Chapter 40:** the hospital cafeteria study, distinguishing sequential labeling/placement changes and sales shares from randomized component effects or health outcomes.

No new figures were needed. Each existing numbered figure and table retains a local prose discussion.

## Lecture coverage and deliberate exclusions

The review used the latest located BE sequence: **10 decks and 677 slides**, alongside the older topic library. The DPN review covered its current **9-deck sequence and 663 slides**, parallel copies, and older influence material. Speaker notes and hidden slides were included in the text extraction. Current sources were compared with actual book passages, not merely with keyword counts or earlier audit conclusions.

The [BE report](be-coverage.md) and its [62-topic ledger](be-coverage.csv), together with the [DPN topic ledger](dpn-coverage.md), identify the chapter destinations and reasons for exclusions. Repeated illustrations, course administration, and specialist clinical excursions were not added solely to reproduce a slide sequence. Unsupported literal claims were qualified or omitted; specific unverified numerical anecdotes were not imported. Lecture slides informed coverage but are not cited as scientific evidence.

This is a substantive topic-coverage review, not certification of every historical slide statistic. Image-heavy topics were checked through notes, selected direct image inspection, published sources, and the existing detailed audits. Not every archived image or embedded video was independently inspected. The ledgers make that limitation explicit rather than treating missing extracted text as proof that a topic is irrelevant.

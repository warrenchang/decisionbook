## Scientific and editorial decisions

- Focusing illusion: attention can give a part disproportionate weight in evaluating a whole. A feature can be real and valuable while its contribution to overall well-being is exaggerated.
- Social comparison changes the benchmark; attentional focus changes the emphasis. They can operate together without being synonymous.
- Focalism identifies the attentional omission in affective forecasts; adaptation describes changes in response after an outcome. Neither implies universal or complete recovery from adverse conditions.
- The existing income and well-being evidence, adaptation boundaries, relationship discussion, and distinction between affect and meaning remain intact.
- The practical application traces both money and time through their consequences. It does not prescribe the lower-paying job or equate value with continuous conscious attention.
- Preserved all existing chapter figures and references. The added Wilson et al. study is the only new bibliographic work; the California study was already present.

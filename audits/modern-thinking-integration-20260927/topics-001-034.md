## Primary-source checks and integration decisions

- **Shin, J., & Ariely, D. (2004).** Keeping doors open: The effect of unavailability on incentives to keep options viable. *Management Science, 50*(5), 575–586. DOI: 10.1287/mnsc.1030.0148. [Full primary paper](https://web.mit.edu/ariely/www/MIT/Papers/doors.pdf). Four paid computer-game experiments; room switches consumed a limited click budget. Disappearing doors prompted extra switching, including when payoff distributions were described. First study alone cannot establish irrationality because exploration supplied information; later conditions addressed knowledge and cost. Ch2.
- **Liu, L., Dehmamy, N., Chown, J., Giles, C. L., & Wang, D. (2021).** Understanding the onset of hot streaks across artistic, cultural, and scientific careers. *Nature Communications, 12*, 5392. DOI: 10.1038/s41467-021-25477-8. [Primary full text](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-25477-8). Career records: stylistic/topic diversity before unusually influential periods, concentration during them. Impact measures differ (auction prices, film ratings, citations); sufficiently long observable careers and correlational design limit prescription. Ch18.
- **Wilson, R. C., Geana, A., White, J. M., Ludvig, E. A., & Cohen, J. D. (2014).** Humans use directed and random exploration to solve the explore–exploit dilemma. *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 143*(6), 2074–2081. DOI: 10.1037/a0038199. [Author-institution full text](https://oar.princeton.edu/bitstream/88435/pr17x6v/1/Humans_Use_Directed_Random_Exploration_2014.pdf). Supplement to source020, useful for experimentally manipulated time horizon. The published task and findings were additionally verified in the primary full text at https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5635655/ before insertion: one versus six free choices after controlled initial observations.
- **Burt, R. S. (2004).** Structural holes and good ideas. *American Journal of Sociology, 110*(2), 349–399. DOI: 10.1086/421787. [Full primary paper](https://snap.stanford.edu/class/cs224w-readings/Burt04StructureHole.pdf). Networks of managers in one electronics company: brokerage associated with better-rated ideas and career rewards. Observational, not random assignment to bridging. Ch34.
- **Chetty et al. (2022a,b).** Social capital I: Measurement and associations with economic mobility; Social capital II: Determinants of economic connectedness. *Nature, 608*, 108–121 and 122–134. DOI: 10.1038/s41586-022-04996-4 and 10.1038/s41586-022-04997-3. [I](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-04996-4), [II](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-04997-3). US Facebook networks reveal distinct bonding and bridging dimensions, area economic connectedness predicts upward mobility, and simple exposure does not ensure friendship. The causal neighborhood analysis does not isolate the effect of making a particular higher-income friend. Ch34.
- **Chetty, R., Hendren, N., & Katz, L. F. (2016).** The effects of exposure to better neighborhoods on children: New evidence from the Moving to Opportunity experiment. *American Economic Review, 106*(4), 855–902. DOI: 10.1257/aer.20150572. [Primary article](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.20150572). Random offer of vouchers for lower-poverty neighborhoods; positive adult income/college outcomes among young children, weaker or adverse results among older children. Supplementary causal boundary for source014. Does not isolate friendships from other neighborhood changes.
- **Bessembinder, H. (2018).** Do stocks outperform Treasury bills? *Journal of Financial Economics, 129*(3), 440–457. DOI: 10.1016/j.jfineco.2018.06.004. [Author institution](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/do-stocks-outperform-treasury-bills/). Historical US individual-stock lifetime returns strongly skewed; best 4% account for aggregate net dollar wealth creation relative to Treasury bills. This is not “only 4% beat bills.” Ch27.
- **Barber, B. M., & Odean, T. (2000).** Trading is hazardous to your wealth: The common stock investment performance of individual investors. *Journal of Finance, 55*(2), 773–806. DOI: 10.1111/0022-1082.00226. [Primary journal](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/0022-1082.00226). 66,465 households, one discount broker, 1991–1996; highest-turnover investors' annual net returns 11.4% versus market 17.9%. Historical commissions matter; observational data do not by themselves prove overconfidence. Ch27.
- **Kelly, J. L., Jr. (1956).** A new interpretation of information rate. *Bell System Technical Journal, 35*(4), 917–926. DOI: 10.1002/j.1538-7305.1956.tb03809.x. [Primary journal](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/j.1538-7305.1956.tb03809.x); [full text mirror](https://homepage.sns.it/marmi/esameIUE/kelly.pdf). Theoretical long-run growth criterion, not empirical investment performance or guaranteed welfare. Ch16 bounded example only.
- **Moser et al. (2017).** Third-person self-talk facilitates emotion regulation without engaging cognitive control: Converging evidence from ERP and fMRI. *Scientific Reports, 7*, 4519. DOI: 10.1038/s41598-017-04047-3. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28674404/). Own-name versus “I” reflection during aversive-image viewing / negative autobiographical recall; differences in emotional reactivity and specified neural markers, not proof no cognitive resources involved. Handoff Ch21.
- **Dale, S. B., & Krueger, A. B. (2002).** Estimating the payoff to attending a more selective college: An application of selection on observables and unobservables. *Quarterly Journal of Economics, 117*(4), 1491–1527. [Primary abstract](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/117/4/1491/1876022). Admission/application-matched comparisons sharply attenuate earnings differences; low-income students show larger returns. Handoff Ch42; the study does not show all colleges have identical effects.
- **Csikszentmihalyi, M., & Getzels, J. W. (1971).** Discovery-oriented behavior and the originality of creative products: A study with artists. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 19*(1), 47–52. DOI: 10.1037/h0031106. [Primary reprint abstract](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-94-017-9085-7_1), explicitly reproducing the 1971 study. Handoff from topic096 owner; verified again before insertion. Observation of 31 advanced art students, from arranging still-life objects through drawing completion; independent expert panel evaluated results. Discovery-oriented behavior during problem formulation correlated with originality, not craftsmanship. Added to Ch02 `#problem-finding-before-problem-solving`; the observational design does not establish that prescribing exploration causes original work.

## Verified source details

- **Milgram (1963), pp. 374–376, procedure and Table 2:** all 40 participants reached 300 volts; 5 stopped there and 26 reached 450. The learner pounded at 300 and 315 volts; the report's proposed early vocal protests and “all went past 300” combine different conditions or misstate the table. The chapter uses the specific 1963 condition and retains later credibility/interpretation qualifications. [Original article PDF](https://web.mit.edu/curhan/www/docs/Articles/15341_Readings/Influence_Compliance/Milgrim_1963_Behavioral_study_of_obedience.pdf).
- **Burger (2009), Table 2:** 28 of 40 baseline participants continued to the next task item after the 150-volt protest, where the experiment stopped them. This is not an observed rate of reaching 450 volts and is not the same Milgram condition as the 1963 wall-pounding procedure. [Original article PDF](https://www.psy.miami.edu/_assets/pdf/rpo-articles/burger-2009.pdf); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0010932).
- **Darley and Latané (1968), Table 1 and response-time discussion:** 85% versus 31% reported the emergency before the recorded speaker's allotted turn ended, with 13 participants in each of the two relevant conditions. At the six-minute cutoff, the corresponding figures were 100% and 62%. The experiment varied believed additional listeners and measured individual helping. The manuscript does not equate those rates with the chance that at least one person in a real crowd helps. [Article DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0025589); [full original article reproduction used for Table 1](https://studylib.net/doc/27122820/darley-and-latane--1968---1-).
- **Fischer et al. (2011):** more than 7,700 participants and 105 independent effect sizes; overall standardized effect g = −0.35. The effect is attenuated in dangerous emergencies, with some physical-support conditions nonnegative. “105 studies” would be an incorrect substitution. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21534650/).
- **Asch (1956), Table 3:** 123 male college students, 36.8% errors on critical comparisons under a wrong unanimous majority; 37 independent controls, 0.7% errors. The percentage is across critical responses, not people who always conformed. [Original monograph scan used](https://www.scribd.com/document/225183280/asch1956).
- **Asch (1955), supporting-partner section:** truthful supporting-partner conditions reduced erroneous responses to approximately one quarter of the unanimous-majority frequency. This is explicitly a condition comparison, not a universal effect of any dissent. [Author's article PDF](https://web.mit.edu/curhan/www/docs/Articles/15341_Readings/Influence_Compliance/Asch_1955_Opinions_and_social_pressure.pdf).
- **Tversky and Kahneman (1981), calculator/jacket Problem 10:** hypothetical $5 saving, 20-minute journey, with the combined two-product price fixed at $140. Travel was chosen by 68% when the calculator was reduced from $15 to $10 (N = 93), versus 29% for $125 to $120 (N = 88). [Original article PDF](https://psych.hanover.edu/classes/Cognition/Papers/tversky81.pdf).
- **Tversky and Kahneman (1992), experiment and parameter-fit sections:** 25 graduate students from Berkeley and Stanford; individual-fit median value exponents 0.88, loss parameter 2.25, probability-weighting exponents 0.61 for gains and 0.69 for losses. [Original article PDF](https://christosaioannou.com/Advances_in_Prospect_Theory.pdf); [full article text used where PDF delivery failed](https://paperzz.com/doc/7911612/advances-in-prospect-theory--cumulative-representation-of...). The manuscript's new lottery example is a model calculation using these illustrative parameters, not an observed preference.
- **Hertwig et al. (2004), pp. 536–537:** median 15 draws per problem across the options. The manuscript no longer assigns a vaguely reported 11–19 range to this experiment. [Original article PDF](https://drupalgsb-dev.cc.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/1329/Weber_RareEvents.pdf).
- **Hertwig and Erev (2009):** the already-listed review discusses three experience paradigms and the contrast in rare-event impact. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19836292/).
- **Watts, Duncan, and Quan (2018):** the main analysis focuses on children whose mothers had not completed college. One additional minute of preschool waiting was associated with about 0.1 SD higher adolescent achievement before controls; the association fell by about two thirds after background, early ability, and home-environment controls. This observational estimate does not identify the effect of teaching patience. [Original article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6050075/).
- **Stasser and Titus (1985):** the original political-caucus simulation distributes partial candidate information. Shared and preference-consistent information can dominate discussion despite collectively available evidence favoring another candidate. [Original article PDF](https://www.uni-muenster.de/imperia/md/content/psyifp/aeechterhoff/vorlesungkommunikation/stasser_titus_unsharedinfogroupdisc_jpsp1985.pdf).

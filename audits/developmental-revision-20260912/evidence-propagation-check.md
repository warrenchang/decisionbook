# Evidence propagation and teaching-prompt integration check

Date: 12 September 2026. This is a read-only check of the revised manuscript, apart from this audit record.

## Exact scope and method

Checked all twenty rows in Appendix F's two evidence-map tables: fourteen direct/coordinated/integrity cases and six attenuated/conditional/reinterpreted cases. Searched all **42 numbered canonical chapter `.qmd` files** for the original authors, update authors, study names, and distinctive claim wording; read matching passages with their neighboring explanation, captions, alt text, and reference entries. Surname-only false positives were examined rather than counted as claims. For example, van Knippenberg in a mimicry reference and O'Donnell in an information-sharing reference are not professor-priming citations.

This audit tests **internal propagation and consistency** with the current Appendix F map. It does not independently recheck every primary paper, retraction notice, or numerical value in that map, and it is not a complete new scientific review of all 42 chapters. Retired, unnumbered chapters, generated HTML/EPUB, and historical audit copies were excluded. A case discussed only in Appendix F does not require a new chapter passage.

Also checked the Chapter 06/Appendix B consolidation, Chapter 12/17/Appendix D division of the outbreak demonstration, and all six callouts currently carrying `.predict-first` in canonical chapter sources.

## The six prediction callouts

The criterion here is an observable commitment made before the relevant information or explanation is revealed. A written choice, a proposed discriminating test, or a forecast can qualify; a general invitation to think about a completed example does not necessarily qualify. No videos were independently viewed in this pass, so the check concerns the written exercise and its sequencing, not a frame-by-frame verification of media content.

| Chapter and callout | Disposition |
| --- | --- |
| 03, “Watch and test: what did your model preserve?” | **Pass as a replay test.** The reader first watches, records which details seemed stable, then checks that record against the replay. The commitment precedes the diagnostic second viewing; this is not presented as a blind prediction before any exposure. |
| 10, “Watch and test: are you testing the rule—or collecting agreement?” | **Commitment requested, setup needs clarification.** It asks for supporting/falsifying observations before opening the video, but “your preferred rule” is not anchored to the video's initial puzzle. A clearer sequence would show the initial puzzle, pause before the explanation, record a candidate rule and a discriminating test, and then continue. |
| 12, “Choose before reading the result” | **Pass.** The reader records a program choice before comparing the complementary frame and seeing the published results below the callout. The written record prevents silently replacing the first response after seeing the second. |
| 26, “Try the social prediction” | **Does not yet meet the stronger criterion.** It asks readers to use three demonstrations/stories to predict counterfactual changes, but does not specify a commitment before opening a demonstration or before a reveal. Either remove the `.predict-first` classification/label while retaining the application, or add a concrete pause–write–continue instruction tied to one clip. |
| 34, “Watch for the mismatch” | **Pass.** It explicitly asks what kind of help each person expects before watching, then supplies a while-watching observation and after-watching response task. |
| 37, “Watch and diagnose: position versus interest” | **Pass.** It directs the reader to pause before the explanation and write positions, possible interests, and a question distinguishing the hypotheses. |

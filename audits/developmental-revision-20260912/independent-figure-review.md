# Independent review of the revised reading figures

Date: 12 September 2026. This was a read-only review of source figures, their generators, current canonical chapter references, and rendered PNGs. Only this audit record was written. The diagram-figure-quality skill guided the review.

That was an unnecessary mediation constraint and did not match the caption's claim that Judgment supports choice. The integrating editor changed the connector to `M205 878 V940`, from the Judgment enclosure to Choice. I reopened the regenerated PNG and confirmed both the intended relation and attachment at the two boundaries. The remaining two-way links within judgment and feedback into Internal State are consistent with the selected-influences caveat.
2. **Resolved on follow-up — `communication-calibration-evidence.svg`.** The two right-panel arrows initially ended at x = 910 while their destination boxes began at x = 920. The integrating editor extended both endpoints to x = 920 and regenerated the PNG. I reopened that PNG and confirmed that both arrowheads meet the target boxes. Their scientific meanings remain reasonable schematic summaries.

## Smaller improvements, not new scientific defects

- The two connecting arrows in `digital-arrow-affordance.svg` are 22 and 26 SVG units long with 12-unit heads. They remain visible in the inspected PNG, but their shafts are short relative to the heads. Smaller markers or a little more connector space would improve their appearance at narrow widths.
- The hotel advertisements are generic illustrations. Their interiors currently differ visually chiefly in the action arrow, while the text correctly says the historical redesign also changed type, spacing, and layout. Describing the display as an **illustration of an action cue within a bundled redesign** would make its status clearer; it should not be read as a faithful pair of experimental stimuli or an isolated-arrow test.
- Chapter 34's alt text still describes a branching map. The revised `conversation-needs-map.svg` uses three separate cards rather than drawn branches. The interpretation is preserved, but “three cards offer possible practical, emotional, and social or identity readings” would describe the visible layout more accurately.
- The Kruger chart legend **receiver confidence** is compatible with the source and the revised caption. A more explicit label would be **receiver judged correct**, because the plotted quantity is a proportion of yes/no judgments after decoding, not a continuous confidence rating. This is a clarity improvement rather than a change to the numbers.

## Scientific checks

**The Chapter 1 benchmark and behavioral map have different purposes.** The first is explicitly an analytical benchmark; the Prediction-to-Valuation sequence should not be treated as a measured sequence of neural or mental events. Its caption and footer make that boundary visible. The second permits interaction among selection, prediction, and valuation and now routes the Judgment enclosure to Choice. Its context/internal-state relations, incomplete influence map, and conditional learning language fit the surrounding discussion.

**BRAIN remains an optional observation practice.** The revised urge figure separates the curve from the prompts and explicitly states that urges can persist or return. No prompt is assigned a peak, duration, or guaranteed relief effect. “Non-identify and nurture” agrees with the chapter's table, which explains it as treating an urge as a passing experience and choosing a caring next action. The figure does not establish the mnemonic as a validated treatment. The chapter's existing footnote retains that boundary.

**Lally's curve and numbers are distinguished.** The top panel is labeled illustrative, has no empirical time coordinates, and depicts diminishing gains. A separate panel gives modeled days to 95% of the plateau: range 18–254, median 66. The caption limits these figures to well-fitted cases, and the footnote notes that some estimates extend beyond observation. The primary paper reports 39 good-fit individuals among 62 fitted models; the displayed range and median therefore must not be generalized to all participants or presented as a deadline. The current chapter does neither. [Primary publication](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674).

**The Chapter 28 diagnostic preserves five separate questions.** Directive authority, suppressed dissent, shared-information bias, pluralistic ignorance, and diffusion of responsibility remain distinct. Each safeguard is framed as something to test, not a guaranteed cure. The private-view versus assumed-group-view question retains the key pluralistic-ignorance distinction. Naming an owner addresses responsibility without being offered as a way to manufacture missing evidence or eliminate dissent.

**The Kruger Study 2 chart matches the verified task and measure.** I read the original Study 2 methods and results and compared them with the chapter worker's source findings. Participants selected statements, predicted which their partner would classify correctly, decoded their partner's messages, and then judged whether each of their own classifications was correct. Receiver values are reported as 89% for email and 91% for voice. The manuscript and chart label the sender/actual values—approximately 78% versus 56% for email and 78% versus 73% for voice—as approximate. Those rounded values were taken from Figure 1 by the chapter worker; I confirmed the published qualitative contrast and chance comparison, without independently digitizing the graph. The analytic sample is 29 dyads after one dyad's exclusion; both media occur within dyads. This is a constrained tone-decoding experiment, not a general error rate for email. [Original article, Study 2, p. 928](https://uploads-ssl.webflow.com/5c484e0f4aa6f839dc553c45/5c86ca1f620e2b5879502a41_KrugerEpleyParkerNg2005.pdf).

The companion perspective-taking/perspective-getting panel is marked by its content and caption as an explanatory schematic. It does not plot another result from the sarcasm experiment or assert that questions always create accurate beliefs.

## Other concrete material removed or moved

Removed examples include the Odysseus, Victor Hugo, and savings-in-bricks anecdotes and an uncited language/saving aside in Chapter 19; the JCPenney pricing detour in Chapter 20; the historical 3DO diagnosis in Chapter 23 (replaced by a generic platform example); three extra Prisoner’s Dilemma film clips in Chapter 25; and the Netflix and unverified 12-percent worked outcome in Chapter 32. These cuts reduce the variety of examples even where the main mechanism remains. They should not all be described as deleted scientific studies.

By contrast, many formal and research extensions were moved into optional notes and remain in the book: expected-utility extensions, related prospect-theory phenomena, experience-based choice extensions, event-study methods, comparative cooperation evidence, and the habit chapter’s computational/addiction notes.

## Editorial assessment

The revision makes the main route easier to follow, but some gains came from narrowing scope. The clearest candidates for optional restoration are the information-gap account of curiosity, the positive-illusions counterpoint, and the distinction between forgiveness and reconciliation/repair, including a carefully framed REACH note. These are distinct ideas. Their deletion was not necessary merely to avoid repetition. The clinical extensions could also be kept as a clearly separated advanced note if they belong within the intended scope of the book.

The mismatched Diamond–Kirkham claim should not be restored as originally written. A superseded status update should not replace the current journal notice. No removed passage or reference has been automatically restored in this turn.

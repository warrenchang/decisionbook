# Study-centered review: Chapters 1–14

Date: 24 September 2026. Scope: the full current bodies, Research Lenses, practice material, and reference lists of canonical Chapters 1–14. Existing uncommitted changes were preserved. This is an editorial review with primary-source verification of the studies newly added or substantially developed below, not a new systematic review of every cited literature.

The governing question was whether readers can follow a substantive finding—what researchers compared, what happened, and what that result permits them to infer—rather than merely receive a named principle or a proposed checklist. Mathematical and normative foundations remain where necessary; their role is not confused with experimental evidence. No existing topic or published reference was removed.

## Primary-source checks for developed passages

- **Baron & Hershey (1988), Chapter 1:** [original paper](https://bear.warrington.ufl.edu/brenner/mar7588/Papers/baron-hershey-jpsp1988.pdf). Evaluations of decisions differ with results even when decision-time information is held fixed. No unverified numerical effect inserted.
- **Bastardi & Shafir (1998), Chapter 2:** [original paper](https://bear.warrington.ufl.edu/brenner/mar7588/Papers/bastardi-jpsp1998.pdf), mortgage example. Checked 29% immediate-condition approval, 75% choosing to wait in the uncertain condition, and 56% overall approval after clarification. The 56% includes early choosers; it is not the approval percentage conditional on waiting.
- **Sana, Weston, & Cepeda (2013), Chapter 3:** [author-hosted paper](https://www.yorku.ca/ncepeda/publications/SWC2013.pdf), both experiments and Tables 1/2. Verified 66/55% with n=20 per group and 73/56% with n=19 per group. Different tasks/conditions prevent a direct comparison of the experiments' effect sizes. Parent agent owns figure construction, uncertainty calculation, and visual QA.
- **Ernst & Banks (2002), Chapter 4:** [original paper](https://www.columbia.edu/cu/cns/resources/ErnstBanks.pdf). Verified virtual-bar height discrimination, visual-noise manipulation, force-feedback haptics, and reliability-dependent cue weights. This supports sensory integration in the task, not every claim of predictive processing.
- **Atlas et al. (2012), Chapter 5:** [primary article and figure descriptions](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22674280/). The behavioral experiment crossed actual opioid delivery and instructions about it; the paper reports separable pain reduction without drug-by-expectancy interaction. No claim that beliefs eliminate or substitute for drug action.
- **Chase & Simon (1973), Chapter 8:** [original paper](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/chess/1973-chase.pdf), methods and first-trial results. Verified three players, five-second viewing, approximately 16/8/4 pieces for actual middle-game positions, and loss of the marked advantage for randomized positions. The passage does not generalize a three-person study into a universal zero expert advantage on random boards.
- **Jones & Harris (1967), Chapter 10:** [primary journal record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0022103167900340), author abstract and original-study description. Verified choice/assignment manipulation and residual attribution. Qualitative result only; no uncertain numeric table values inserted.
- **Fazio et al. (2015), Chapter 13:** [original paper](https://bear.warrington.ufl.edu/brenner/mar7588/Papers/fazio-jepgen2015.pdf), Experiment 1. Verified initial interest ratings, repeated/new truth judgments, and a subsequent knowledge check. Knowledge still improved accuracy; repetition also influenced judgments for known falsehoods.
- **Hoffrage & Gigerenzer (1998), Chapter 14:** [primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9609869/) and [original article](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=5afaaa6547e10c6ca7108539c3e55991f98ebc9b&repid=rep1&type=pdf). Verified 48 physicians, four diagnostic problems, and 10%/46% Bayesian solutions under the two formats. The book's following disease figures remain explicitly hypothetical and are not attributed to this experiment.

## Source checks and scientific boundaries

- [Pinker, Nowak, and Lee (2008), publisher text](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0707192105) explicitly presents the Emperor's New Clothes as a common-knowledge illustration. The chapter presents it as a parable, not an experimental result.
- [Aumann (1976), author-hosted abstract](https://ma.huji.ac.il/raumann/pdf/Agreeing%20to%20Disagree.htm) supplies the iterated-knowledge definition; [publication DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176343654). No claim about Aumann's agreement theorem was added.
- [Lewis, publisher description of the reissue](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/book/10.1002/9780470693711) confirms the original 1969 publication and the coordination account of convention. [Authorized excerpt and introduction](https://econjwatch.org/file_download/1119/LewisSept2019.pdf?mimetype=pdf) identifies the common-knowledge discussion at original pp. 52–60. The bibliography retains the original Harvard edition rather than assigning the reissue's date to it.
- [Rubinstein (1989), author-hosted paper](https://arielrubinstein.tau.ac.il/papers/32.pdf), [author's publication list](https://arielrubinstein.tau.ac.il/publications.html), and [article abstract and metadata](https://ideas.repec.org/a/aea/aecrev/v79y1989i3p385-91.html) support the contrast between arbitrarily many finite levels of knowledge and common knowledge. The chapter confines the equilibrium conclusion to the model's payoff and communication assumptions; it does not claim that all unreliable communication prevents coordination.
- [Chwe, Princeton book record](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt24hqf1) verifies the original 2001 date and publisher; [the author's Applications chapter in the later edition](https://academic.oup.com/princeton-scholarship-online/book/13454/chapter-abstract/166927123) describes rituals, visible attention, and common-knowledge generation. The chapter attributes this interpretation to Chwe and does not claim experimentally identified effects of meetings or ceremonies.

Common knowledge is scoped to a particular fact. Private and shared receipt are distinguished from a public announcement whose reception and meaning are themselves commonly recognized. Real meetings and alarms are examples of improving evidence about others' knowledge, not guarantees of the formal infinite hierarchy. Common knowledge neither selects an equilibrium by itself nor assures compliance; successful coordination need not require perfect common knowledge.

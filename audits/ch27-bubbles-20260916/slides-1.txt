## Slide 12 (hidden=False)
Many traders care less about fundamentals and more about the price they expect to sell for in the future.
Greater fool theory
: investors may knowingly buy overvalued assets, believing they can still sell them later at a higher price to a “greater fool.”
Even if traders recognize that prices are misaligned with fundamentals, they may believe they can sell quickly enough before a crash occurs.
However, when a crash does happen, there are no buyers at any price level.

## Slide 41 (hidden=False)
Trading strategies and experimental bubbles
Passive traders
: buy when prices are below fundamental value, sell when above (value trader)
Expect reversion to intrinsic value (rational arbitrage), stabilize the market.
Feedback traders
: increase demand when prices are rising (trend follower)
Extrapolate recent trends, amplifies price deviations and bubble growth
Speculative traders
: buy if prices are expected to continue rising, regardless of fundamentals (believing greater fool theory)
Expect to resell at higher prices before the crash, sustains bubbles
40
Sources: De Long et al. (1990); Haruvy & Noussair (2006)

Real-time uncertainty makes bubbles difficult to identify before they collapse.
Laboratory bubbles emerge even when values and trading horizons are common knowledge.

There is no universal liquidity-ratio cutoff at which a bubble must occur.

## Slide 58 (hidden=True)
3Com and Palm
March 2, 2000: 3Com carves out in an IPO 5% of its subsidiary Palm.
At same time 3Com announced that in the near future the remaining 95% of the shares would be distributed to current shareholders (roughly 1.5 of Palm/share of 3Com).
Two ways of buying Palm:
Buy Palm directly.
Buy 3Com getting Palm and rest of 3Com business.

## Slide 61 (hidden=True)
What can explain this?
“Smart” investors were limited in their ability to short-sell Palm (as documented in Lamont and Thaler), so it wasn’t their fault.
But this cannot explain why anybody would buy Palm instead of 3Com – for this one needs irrationality.
In facts 2 things are needed for mispricing to exist:
Irrational investors
Limits to arbitrage (here due to implementation costs)
3Com & Palm case illustrates that mispricing does not imply a free lunch!
60
Source: Lamont & Thaler (2003)

# Independent review: Chapter 30 elaboration quadrant

Date: 25 September 2026. Scope: `figures/elaboration-quadrant.svg`, its 1140 × 1140 PNG fallback, and the integrating paragraph, caption, alternative text, and Table 30.1 in `chapters/30-persuasion.qmd`.

## Scientific meaning — PASS

- The horizontal axis is ability to evaluate a message, not physical capacity to carry out the recommended behavior. The prose correctly includes knowledge, time, and attention.
- The vertical axis is motivation to evaluate. The diagram distinguishes wanting to examine a proposal from having the opportunity and resources to do so.
- Greater scrutiny is made more likely when both dimensions are high. The mixed quadrants identify different constraints instead of suggesting that high motivation alone or high ability alone ensures extensive processing.
- No quadrant promises agreement, truth, accuracy, or an intrinsically better attitude. The surrounding text retains the possibility of motivated, biased elaboration.
- The caption identifies continuous dimensions rather than discrete, permanent audience types. Dashed midlines and Low/High endpoints supply a schematic comparison without inventing numerical thresholds or an estimated response surface.
- The new figure is locally discussed and immediately accompanies the corresponding table. The table supplies practical detail rather than forcing it into the graphic.

Source comparison: Petty and Briñol (2012), original chapter extracted by the root agent to `/private/tmp/petty-brinol-2012-elm.txt`, especially printed pages 228–229 and 232. The source describes extent of thinking as dependent on motivation and ability; specifies knowledge, time, and opportunity as relevant limits; and states that high elaboration need not produce an unbiased attitude. This reviewer checked those passages against the diagram; source retrieval and bibliographic verification were performed by the root agent.

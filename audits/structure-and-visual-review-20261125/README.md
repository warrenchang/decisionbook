# Structure and figure review of Decision in the Making

**25 November 2026**

Keep the seven-part progression. The most consequential revision is to let a few decisions develop across the book and give each mechanism, study and tool one full explanation. The hiring vote in the Preface already establishes the book’s central question. By the end, readers should be able to reconstruct how that vote formed, identify what could have changed it, and design a process that would let the committee learn from the next hire.

The choice of what to illustrate follows from that structure. The book is strongest when an image lets readers make a judgment, compare evidence or see a relationship they would have to work to reconstruct. Too many other images turn a paragraph or table into boxes. The review proposes **converting 28 numbered placements to prose or native tables and consolidating eight others**. Another 15 merit redesign and 10 should move. Forty-seven already make a useful visual contribution in their current location.

4. **Draw relationships that change the decision.** Use Chapter 14’s two applicant pools to show why the same screen yields a posterior of 40% in one pool and about 72.7% in the other, under the chapter’s assumed rates. Draw the positive denominator from true and false positives. In Chapter 20 lay out the same payments at two dates of evaluation so the reversal is visible. Keep the discount curves in the formal treatment. Leave the many helpful illustrations in place, especially the sensory worlds, cold-water protocol, map projections and action thresholds.

## Two sample treatments

**Replace the priming pathway with a comparison.** In Meyer and Schvaneveldt’s experiment, two strings appeared together. Readers decided whether both were words and responded faster to associated pairs such as *nurse* and *doctor*. A sequential test asks a different question. Neely presented a cue before the target and found faster word decisions after related primes than after a neutral warning cue. When the target is held fixed, that contrast isolates the role of the preceding cue in the task. A frame can instead change how the current problem itself is presented. [Meyer and Schvaneveldt (1971)](https://labs.la.utexas.edu/gilden/files/2016/04/MeyerSchvaneveldt1971.pdf) · [Neely (1976)](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/BF03213230.pdf)

**Give the bargaining plot the actual menu.** Which package would both sides choose over walking away? Start with the two thresholds, then inspect the four points. Short and long refer to contract length. Fixed and flexible refer to delivery quantities.

![Four hypothetical buyer–supplier packages. A vertical line at supplier score 45 and a horizontal line at buyer score 40 mark the walk-away values. Only the long contract with flexible quantities clears both, improving both scores by 40 points over the short contract with fixed quantities.](package-scores-prototype.png)

Each point is one of the chapter’s four hypothetical packages. Moving from short/fixed to long/flexible raises each party’s own score from 30 to 70. Three packages are nondominated within this menu. Only long/flexible beats both outside options. The scores are party-specific. ## Revision order

1. **Set the reading path.** Revise the Preface and Chapter 1 together, bring the Chapter 4 reveal into the end of the core explanation, and give the duplicated examples a single developed home. Update the case map and part introductions as references move.
2. **Test four visual edits.** Use the table in Chapter 18, the dialogue in Chapter 33, the paired dated choices in Chapter 20 and the package plot in Chapter 37. Check that each still lets a reader answer the chapter’s question and that references resolve in both editions.
3. **Apply the chapter plan.** Start with Chapters 27, 29 and 39, then work through the companion chapters and appendices. Preserve the competing evidence and material qualifications when moving content into extensions. Be explicit whether an image presents observations, a model, a hypothetical example or a simulation.
4. **Verify the revised editions.** Preserve legacy anchors and update figure and table references, alt text, captions, examples, assessments and the bibliography. Rebuild HTML and EPUB. Check representative pages at desktop and phone widths and inspect any figure that moves or changes.

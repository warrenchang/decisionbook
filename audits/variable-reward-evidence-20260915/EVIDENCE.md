# Variable-reward evidence added to Chapter 21

Verified 2026-09-15. This is a targeted teaching synthesis, not a systematic review. Full citations are in the chapter and synchronized master references.

| Source and primary evidence | Design and measure | Interpretation boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Anselme et al. (2013)](https://robinsonlab.research.wesleyan.edu/files/2014/01/Anselme-2013-Behav-Brain-Res.pdf), pp. 54–56 | Experiment 1: 56 rats, four groups; lever/dish response ratio. | Combined probability/amount manipulation; no group difference in absolute presses. Initial sign-tracker selection; full-sample ratio comparison also reported. |
| [Bateson & Kacelnik (1995)](https://www.staff.ncl.ac.uk/melissa.bateson/Bateson_Kacelnik_1995a.pdf), pp. 313, 322 | Starlings; within-subject amount/delay treatments; choice, latency, pecking. | Amount-choice result null despite other indices. Programmed average rates were matched; mean delays were not. |
| [Bromberg-Martin & Hikosaka (2009)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19607797/), abstract and figures 1–2 | Two macaques; advance-information choices. | Evidence of information seeking, not conscious pattern discovery. Accessible primary abstract/figure descriptions used; no unsupported task parameters added. |
| [Shen et al. (2015)](https://luxi-shen-4unh.squarespace.com/s/2015_ShenFishbachHsee_Uncertainty_GoalPersuit_JCR.pdf), studies 1, 3–4 | Human reward pursuit; task completion, bids, process/outcome focus. | Magnitude uncertainty; excitement ratings, no dopamine measurement. Do not equate with reward omission or established habit. |

The synthesis compares cue attraction, option choice, and information seeking before drawing the human parallel. It does not infer a universal preference for uncertainty or an optimal 50% probability. No original study figures or raw data were altered.
